階段 6 / 7

如何防治 TDDB

把使用端 1 ppm 的壽命公式攤開來，你能動的手只有幾隻。 先看清楚哪幾隻，再談製程、元件、電路各能出多少力。

Stage 6 / 7

How to mitigate TDDB

Unfold the lifetime formula for the 1-ppm use-end target and there are only a handful of knobs you can actually turn. See which ones they are first, then look at how much leverage process, device, and circuit design each have.

全部的槓桿都在這一行裡
t1ppm = t₀ · exp(−γ·Eox) · exp(Ea/kBT) · ( Atest/A )1/β · ( 10−6 )1/β

紅色是要壓小的，藍色是要拉大的。整份對策清單就是這四項的展開：

① 降 Eox= V / tox 降電壓，或在同樣的 EOT 下把物理厚度做厚（氮化、high-k）。指數項，槓桿最大。
② 拉大 β= tox/a₀ 物理厚度變厚，分布收窄，兩道外推的罰款同時變輕。跟 ① 是同一個動作的副產品。
③ 縮小 A閘極總面積 設計端的事。大面積 MOS decap 常常是整顆晶片 TDDB 的主要貢獻者。
④ 降 T使用溫度 Ea ≈ 0.75 eV，降 20 °C 大約只有 2 倍。槓桿最小，但常常免費。
⑤ 殺外質不在式子裡 上面整條式子講的是本質壽命。外質失效（針孔、微粒、電漿損傷）另有一條分布， 而且它會完全支配 ppm 級的判定。這一項是 foundry 的主戰場。
Every lever is in this one line
t1ppm = t₀ · exp(−γ·Eox) · exp(Ea/kBT) · ( Atest/A )1/β · ( 10−6 )1/β

Red is what you want to shrink; blue is what you want to grow. The entire mitigation list is just these four terms spelled out:

① Lower Eox= V / tox Lower the voltage, or make the physical thickness thicker at the same EOT (nitridation, high-k). It's in the exponent, so this has the biggest leverage.
② Raise β= tox/a₀ A thicker physical layer narrows the distribution, lightening both extrapolation penalties at once. A free side effect of ①.
③ Shrink ATotal gate area A design-side lever. A large-area MOS decap is often the single biggest contributor to a whole chip's TDDB.
④ Lower TUse temperature Ea ≈ 0.75 eV, so dropping 20 °C only buys about 2×. Smallest leverage, but often free.
⑤ Kill extrinsicsNot in the formula The whole formula above describes the intrinsic lifetime. Extrinsic failures (pinholes, particles, plasma damage) follow a separate distribution that can completely dominate the ppm-level verdict. This is the foundry's main battleground.

先看第 ⑤ 項：為什麼外質缺陷會讓前面四項全部白做

假設你的本質壽命非常漂亮，但每一萬顆裡有 2 顆的閘極氧化層有個薄點。 那 1 ppm 的判定會落在哪一條分布上？

Look at item ⑤ first: why extrinsic defects can waste all the effort in the other four

Suppose your intrinsic lifetime looks beautiful, but 2 out of every 10,000 devices has a thin spot in the gate oxide. Which distribution does the 1-ppm verdict actually land on?

看清楚這件事：只要外質不良比例高於你的目標失效率，那條線就完全由外質決定。 此時再怎麼改善本質壽命（加厚、降壓）都不會讓判定變好 —— 你要做的是把外質模式消掉（製程潔淨度、antenna 規則）或篩掉它（burn-in）。 反過來說，外質已經降到目標失效率以下之後，本質壽命才重新變成瓶頸。 搞錯自己在哪一個區間，是 TDDB 改善計畫最常見的浪費。

對策一：製程

製程

① 缺陷密度控制 —— 潔淨度、微粒、金屬污染

閘極氧化層的外質崩潰，絕大多數來自成長前的表面狀態：微粒、有機殘留、金屬離子（Fe、Cu、Ni）。 這是 foundry 唯一能真正把外質模式壓到 ppm 以下的手段，也是最不性感但最有效的一項。

怎麼知道有沒有效：V-Ramp / J-Ramp 的 wafer map。 外質失效會顯示為邊緣或特定區塊的低 VBD 群聚；本質失效則是全片均勻。

製程

② 氮化（SiON）—— 在同樣 EOT 下把物理厚度做厚

氮進入氧化層把介電常數從 3.9 拉到 4.2～4.8，於是同樣的 EOT 對應更厚的物理膜， 電場下降、β 上升，TDDB 兩頭受惠。順帶擋住 PMOS 閘極的硼穿透。

代價：界面附近的氮會明顯惡化 NBTI。 所以現代製程用的是電漿氮化 + 後退火（DPN + PNA），把氮的分布往上表面推、遠離 Si/SiO₂ 界面。 「氮往哪裡放」是 TDDB 與 NBTI 之間的直接取捨。

製程

③ High-k / metal gate —— 但它不會自動改善 TDDB

這是最常被誤解的一項。高介電常數讓你在同樣 EOT 下把物理膜做得很厚，漏電大幅下降 —— 但界面層（IL）那層 SiO₂ 感受到的電場，仍然是 V / EOT。 因為串聯電容的位移場連續，EIL / Ehk = khk / kIL， 薄的低 k 界面層反而承受最高的電場。而 TDDB 的貫穿路徑通常就是先在 IL 形成。

所以：high-k 的 TDDB 好處來自「可以用更厚的 IL 而不犧牲驅動力」與「總路徑更長」， 不是來自降電場。而 HfO₂ 本身有大量氧空缺陷（VO）， 其缺陷生成率與 SiO₂ 完全不同，β 與加速參數必須重新量，不能沿用 SiO₂ 的。

製程

④ 氧化製程本身：ISSG / RTO vs 爐管

快速熱氧化與 in-situ steam generation 給出較低的界面態密度與較好的厚度均勻性。 厚度均勻性直接影響 β 的量測 —— 厚度分布會被誤讀成分布變散。

製程

⑤ 退火與鈍化（forming gas / 氘）

H₂ 或 D₂ 退火鈍化 Si/SiO₂ 界面的懸鍵，降低初始缺陷密度。 氘的優勢在於較重、較難被熱載子解離（對 HCI 效果顯著）；對 TDDB 主要是降低初始缺陷。

製程

⑥ 電漿充電損傷與 antenna 規則

金屬蝕刻與電漿製程期間，大面積金屬會收集電荷並全部灌進與它相連的閘極 —— 在還沒出廠前就先注入了一堆缺陷。這是外質失效的重要來源。

對策：設計規則裡的 antenna ratio 限制（金屬面積 / 閘極面積上限）、 加保護二極體、把長金屬用 via 跳到上層再接回。這條規則是 foundry 開給設計端的，違反了 foundry 也救不了。

對策二：元件設計

元件

⑦ 雙閘氧（dual gate oxide）

核心邏輯用薄氧追效能，I/O 與高壓節點用厚氧扛電壓。 這是最直接也最普遍的作法 —— 因為 tox 同時改善 Eox 與 β 兩項。

代價：多一道光罩與氧化步驟；厚氧元件的驅動力與面積都變差。

元件

⑧ 閘極總面積管理 —— 別忘了 decap

TDDB 的失效率跟面積直接相關。一顆晶片上面積最大的閘極通常不是邏輯閘，是 MOS decap， 而且它 24 小時全時間承受完整的 VDD。 很多「莫名其妙的 TDDB 失效」追到最後都是 decap。

對策：改用 MOM / MIM 電容，或用厚氧元件做 decap，或串接兩顆分壓。

元件

⑨ 疊接分壓（stacked / cascode devices）

兩顆元件串聯，每顆只承受一半的電壓。因為壽命對電場是指數相依， 電壓減半換到的壽命增益遠遠超過面積加倍的損失。

代價：面積、速度、以及中間節點的偏壓需要額外電路維持。

元件

⑩ 圖形與邊角效應

STI 邊角、閘極端點、以及任何曲率大的地方，氧化層會變薄且電場集中。 在版圖上這些是「局部的薄氧化層」，會在 Weibull 圖上表現為早夭。

對策：圓角化的 STI 頂角製程、避免在有源區邊界擺最小尺寸閘極、 DRC 加上閘極端點延伸（gate extension）規則。

對策三：電路與使用條件

電路

⑪ 降低工作電壓 —— 永遠是第一對策

壽命對電場是指數相依。以 γ = 2 cm/MV、tox = 3 nm 計， 降 0.3 V 就換到約 7 倍壽命；相較之下降 20 °C 只有約 2 倍。 DVFS（動態電壓頻率調節）除了省電，也是在省 TDDB 壽命。

電路

⑫ 上電時序與突波 —— 瞬間比穩態更傷

因為指數相依，一個 10% 的過壓突波所造成的損傷，遠大於它佔的時間比例。 上電順序錯了造成閘極先於基板到位、ESD 事件、感性負載的反衝，都是 TDDB 的實際殺手。

對策：power sequencing、soft-start、箝位二極體、 以及在可靠度模擬時沿真實波形積分而不是只看直流工作點 —— 跟 HCI 的 Age function 完全同一個道理。

電路

⑬ Charge pump 與高壓節點

非揮發記憶體的寫入電壓、LDO 的 pass device、level shifter 的跨域節點， 這些地方的閘極電壓經常超過標準 VDD。這些節點必須單獨列出來做 TDDB 預算。

電路

⑭ Burn-in：只篩外質，不改本質

高溫高壓短時間，把外質早夭品燒出來。對本質 TDDB 完全沒有幫助 —— 本質壽命還有幾十年，burn-in 只消耗掉可忽略的一小段。

代價：成本、以及 burn-in 本身也在傷好品。 條件必須用同一套加速模型算過，確保「燒掉外質」遠快於「傷到本質」。

電路

⑮ 溫度管理

Ea ≈ 0.75 eV，槓桿小（降 20 °C ≈ 2 倍），但通常是散熱設計的免費附贈。 值得注意的是它跟 HCI 方向相反 —— 把晶片弄冷會改善 TDDB 但惡化 HCI。

檢查你懂了嗎

主管要你「把 TDDB 壽命提升 10 倍」，預算只夠做一件事。你會先看什麼？
先看現在是外質支配還是本質支配。把 V-Ramp / J-Ramp 的 wafer map 與定電壓 TDDB 的 Weibull 圖攤開來：如果 Weibull 圖左下角有明顯翹起的早夭群（β 遠低於 tox/a₀），那是外質，錢要花在潔淨度、antenna 規則、電漿損傷；如果分布是乾淨的單一直線，才是本質問題，錢要花在加厚 / 降壓。順序搞反的話，改善本質壽命 10 倍在 ppm 判定上會完全看不到效果。
為什麼 high-k 讓漏電降了三個數量級，TDDB 卻沒有等比例改善？
因為串聯介電層的位移場連續：EIL/Ehk = khk/kIL，薄的低 k 界面層承受的電場最高，而且 EIL 其實就等於 V/EOT —— 跟同樣 EOT 的純 SiO₂ 一模一樣。漏電是穿隧問題（看物理厚度，high-k 大勝），TDDB 是界面層電場與缺陷生成問題（看 EOT，沒有變好）。兩個機制看的是不同的厚度，這是 high-k 最容易被誤解的地方。
氮化可以同時降電場又拉大 β，聽起來全是好處。為什麼不氮化到底？
因為界面附近的氮會顯著惡化 NBTI（產生更多可被電洞活化的界面態前驅物）。TDDB 與 NBTI 在「氮放多少、放哪裡」這件事上是直接對打的。現代做法是電漿氮化加後退火，把氮往遠離 Si/SiO₂ 界面的方向推，取一個折衷。這是可靠度工程的常態：沒有單向的改善，只有取捨的位置。
電路端說「我降 0.1 V 就好」，這對 TDDB 有多少用？
算一下就知道：E-model 下壽命 ∝ exp(−γE)，γ = 2 cm/MV。tox = 3 nm 時，降 0.1 V 等於降 0.33 MV/cm，壽命 ×exp(0.67) ≈ 2 倍。對照之下，降 20 °C 也只有約 1.8 倍。0.1 V 就抵得上 20 °C 的散熱工程 —— 這就是為什麼要先去談電壓，再去談散熱。
Burn-in 條件開得越兇越好嗎？
不是。burn-in 用的是同一套加速模型，它同時在加速外質和本質。條件太兇時，外質確實燒得快，但好品也被消耗掉不可忽略的壽命，甚至可能製造出新的缺陷（尤其電壓拉太高進入不同的損傷機制）。正確做法是用外質與本質的兩條加速曲線算出一個窗口：在該條件下外質失效時間 ≪ burn-in 時間 ≪ 本質壽命的可忽略比例。

可調模型

隱私：純靜態頁面，全部運算在你的瀏覽器本機執行 —— 無後端、無 cookie、無追蹤。教育用途，使用者自負驗證責任。
Look closely at this: as long as the extrinsic defect fraction is higher than your target failure rate, that line is entirely determined by extrinsics. At that point, no amount of improving the intrinsic lifetime (thicker oxide, lower voltage) will improve the verdict — what you need to do is eliminate the extrinsic mode (process cleanliness, antenna rules) or screen it out (burn-in). Conversely, once extrinsics drop below the target failure rate, the intrinsic lifetime becomes the bottleneck again. Not knowing which regime you're in is the single most common waste in a TDDB improvement effort.

Countermeasure set 1: Process

Process

① Defect-density control — cleanliness, particles, metal contamination

Extrinsic gate-oxide breakdown overwhelmingly traces back to the surface state before growth: particles, organic residue, metal ions (Fe, Cu, Ni). This is the only lever a foundry has that can genuinely push the extrinsic mode below ppm — and it's the least glamorous but most effective one.

How to tell if it's working: V-Ramp / J-Ramp wafer maps. Extrinsic failures show up as clusters of low VBD at the edge or in specific regions; intrinsic failures are uniform across the whole wafer.

Process

② Nitridation (SiON) — thicken the physical layer at the same EOT

Nitrogen incorporated into the oxide pulls the dielectric constant from 3.9 up to 4.2–4.8, so the same EOT now corresponds to a thicker physical film: the field drops and β rises, benefiting TDDB both ways. As a bonus, it also blocks boron penetration into the PMOS gate.

Cost: nitrogen near the interface noticeably worsens NBTI. That's why modern processes use plasma nitridation + post-nitridation anneal (DPN + PNA), pushing nitrogen toward the top surface, away from the Si/SiO₂ interface. "Where the nitrogen sits" is a direct trade-off between TDDB and NBTI.

Process

③ High-k / metal gate — but it does not automatically improve TDDB

This is the most commonly misunderstood item. A high dielectric constant lets you build a physically thick film at the same EOT, cutting leakage dramatically — but the field felt by the interfacial layer (IL) of SiO₂ is still just V / EOT. Because the displacement field is continuous across series capacitors, EIL / Ehk = khk / kIL, so the thin, low-k interfacial layer actually carries the highest field. And TDDB's percolation path usually forms in the IL first.

So: high-k's TDDB benefit comes from "being able to use a thicker IL without sacrificing drive current" and "a longer total path," not from lowering the field. HfO₂ itself also has a high density of oxygen-vacancy defects (VO), whose generation rate differs completely from SiO₂'s, so β and the acceleration parameters must be re-measured — they can't just be carried over from SiO₂.

Process

④ The oxidation process itself: ISSG / RTO vs. furnace

Rapid thermal oxidation and in-situ steam generation give lower interface-trap density and better thickness uniformity. Thickness uniformity directly affects the β measurement — a spread of thickness can be misread as a wider distribution.

Process

⑤ Anneal and passivation (forming gas / deuterium)

H₂ or D₂ anneal passivates dangling bonds at the Si/SiO₂ interface, lowering the initial defect density. Deuterium's advantage is that it's heavier and harder for hot carriers to dissociate (a large effect for HCI); for TDDB it mainly just lowers the initial defect count.

Process

⑥ Plasma-induced charging damage and antenna rules

During metal etch and plasma steps, a large area of metal collects charge and dumps all of it into whichever gate it's connected to — injecting a batch of defects before the part has even shipped. This is a major source of extrinsic failure.

Countermeasure: the antenna-ratio limit in design rules (a cap on metal area / gate area), adding protection diodes, and routing long metal runs up through a via to a higher layer before coming back down. This rule is issued by the foundry to the design side — violating it, even the foundry can't save you.

Countermeasure set 2: Device design

Device

⑦ Dual gate oxide

Thin oxide for core logic chasing performance, thick oxide for I/O and high-voltage nodes carrying the voltage. This is the most direct and most common approach — because tox improves both Eox and β at once.

Cost: an extra mask and oxidation step; the thick-oxide device gives up drive current and area.

Device

⑧ Total gate-area management — don't forget decoupling capacitors

TDDB's failure rate scales directly with area. On most chips, the single largest gate area usually isn't a logic gate — it's the MOS decap, and it sits under the full VDD around the clock. Many "mysterious TDDB failures" trace back to decap, once you dig far enough.

Countermeasure: switch to MOM / MIM capacitors, use a thick-oxide device for decap, or stack two in series to split the voltage.

Device

⑨ Stacked / cascode devices

Two devices in series, each carrying half the voltage. Because lifetime depends exponentially on field, the lifetime gain from halving the voltage vastly outweighs the cost of doubling the area.

Cost: area, speed, and the extra circuitry needed to hold the mid-node bias steady.

Device

⑩ Layout and corner effects

STI corners, gate edges, and anywhere with high curvature — the oxide there tends to thin out and the field concentrates. On a layout, these are "locally thin oxide" spots, and they show up on a Weibull plot as early failures.

Countermeasure: rounded STI corner processes, avoiding minimum-size gates right at active-area boundaries, and DRC rules that require gate-extension margins at gate ends.

Countermeasure set 3: Circuit and use condition

Circuit

⑪ Lower the operating voltage — always the first countermeasure

Lifetime depends exponentially on field. With γ = 2 cm/MV and tox = 3 nm, dropping 0.3 V buys about 7× lifetime; by comparison, dropping 20 °C only buys about 2×. DVFS (dynamic voltage-frequency scaling) saves power, but it's also saving TDDB lifetime.

Circuit

⑫ Power-up sequencing and surges — transients hurt more than steady state

Because of the exponential dependence, the damage from a 10% overvoltage surge is far larger than the fraction of time it occupies. A gate reaching its final voltage before the substrate due to bad power-up ordering, ESD events, and inductive-load kickback are all real TDDB killers.

Countermeasure: power sequencing, soft-start, clamp diodes, and integrating along the actual waveform during reliability simulation rather than only looking at the DC operating point — exactly the same logic as HCI's age function.

Circuit

⑬ Charge pumps and high-voltage nodes

Non-volatile memory write voltages, an LDO's pass device, the cross-domain node of a level shifter — the gate voltage at these spots often exceeds the standard VDD. These nodes must be budgeted for TDDB separately.

Circuit

⑭ Burn-in: screens extrinsics only, doesn't change intrinsics

Short-duration high temperature and voltage burns out extrinsic early failures. It does nothing at all for intrinsic TDDB — the intrinsic lifetime is still decades away; burn-in only consumes a negligible sliver of it.

Cost: cost itself, plus burn-in also damages good parts. Conditions must be worked out from the same acceleration model to make sure "burning out extrinsics" happens far faster than "damaging intrinsics."

Circuit

⑮ Thermal management

Ea ≈ 0.75 eV, so the leverage is small (dropping 20 °C ≈ 2×), but it's often a free byproduct of thermal design anyway. Worth noting: it points opposite to HCI — making the chip colder improves TDDB but worsens HCI.

Check your understanding

Your manager wants "10× TDDB lifetime," but the budget only covers one change. What do you check first?
Whether you're currently extrinsic-dominated or intrinsic-dominated. Lay out the V-Ramp / J-Ramp wafer map alongside the constant-voltage TDDB Weibull plot: if the Weibull plot shows an upturned early-death cluster at the lower left (β far below tox/a₀), that's extrinsic — spend on cleanliness, antenna rules, plasma-damage control. If the distribution is a clean single line, it's an intrinsic problem — spend on a thicker oxide or lower voltage. Get the order backward and a 10× improvement in intrinsic lifetime will show up as zero improvement in the ppm verdict.
Why doesn't high-k improve TDDB proportionally, even though it drops leakage by three orders of magnitude?
Because the displacement field is continuous across series dielectrics: EIL/Ehk = khk/kIL, so the thin low-k interfacial layer carries the highest field, and EIL actually just equals V/EOT — identical to plain SiO₂ at the same EOT. Leakage is a tunneling problem (depends on physical thickness, where high-k wins big); TDDB is an interfacial-layer field and defect-generation problem (depends on EOT, which hasn't improved). The two mechanisms look at different thicknesses — this is where high-k is most easily misunderstood.
Nitridation lowers the field and raises β at the same time — sounds like pure upside. Why not nitride to the max?
Because nitrogen near the interface significantly worsens NBTI (it creates more interface-trap precursors that holes can activate). TDDB and NBTI pit directly against each other over "how much nitrogen, and where." The modern approach is plasma nitridation plus a post-anneal that pushes nitrogen away from the Si/SiO₂ interface — a compromise. This is the norm in reliability engineering: there's no one-directional improvement, only a choice of where to sit on the trade-off.
The circuit team says "we can only spare 0.1 V." How much does that actually help TDDB?
Just calculate it: under the E-model, lifetime ∝ exp(−γE), γ = 2 cm/MV. At tox = 3 nm, dropping 0.1 V is a 0.33 MV/cm field drop, so lifetime ×exp(0.67) ≈ 2×. For comparison, dropping 20 °C only buys about 1.8×. 0.1 V is worth about as much as 20 °C of thermal engineering — which is why voltage should always be discussed before cooling.
Is a harsher burn-in condition always better?
No. Burn-in runs on the same acceleration model, so it accelerates extrinsics and intrinsics simultaneously. Push the conditions too hard and extrinsics do burn out faster, but good parts also lose a non-negligible chunk of lifetime — and may even develop new defects (especially if the voltage is pushed high enough to trigger a different damage mechanism). The right approach is to use the two acceleration curves for extrinsic and intrinsic to find a window where: extrinsic failure time ≪ burn-in duration ≪ a negligible fraction of the intrinsic lifetime.
