階段 6 / 7 · 如何防治

怎麼把 HCI 壓下去

前面五頁在解釋「為什麼會壞」。這一頁講「怎麼不讓它壞」—— 分成製程、元件、電路三個層級，每一項都附上代價。 所有 HCI 對策都是取捨，沒有免費的。

一分鐘版
  • 所有對策最終都在做同一件事：把 Em = (VD−Vdsat)/l 壓下去， 不是把分子變小，就是把分母變大。
  • 製程端最有效的是 LDD（n⁻ 延伸） —— 拉長 l，本模型下改善約 700 倍，Idsat 只損失幾 %。
  • pocket / halo 會惡化 HCI（縮短 l、抬高 Em），但它是治短通道效應必要的手段 —— 這是最典型的兩難。
  • 電路端最有效的是降低汲極擺幅（指數效益）與 cascode 分壓。
  • 界面本身也能改：氮化用 Si–N 取代 Si–H、氘退火用 D 取代 H，都是直接提高 φit。

先看全貌：每個對策動的是哪一項

Em ≈ VD − Vdsat l   ，   損傷率 ∝ (ID/W) · e−φit / (qλEm) 只有四個地方可以下手：壓低分子（降 VD、抬 Vdsat）、 拉大分母（增大 l）、提高 φit（換更強的鍵）、 或縮短暴露時間（duty、轉態時間）。下面每一個對策都可以歸到這四類之一。

Stage 6 / 7 · Mitigation

Pushing HCI down

The last five pages explained "why it breaks." This page covers "how to stop it from breaking" — split into three levels, process, device, and circuit, each countermeasure with its cost attached. Every HCI countermeasure is a trade-off; none of them are free.

The 1-minute version
  • Every countermeasure ultimately does the same thing: push down Em = (VD−Vdsat)/l, either by shrinking the numerator or growing the denominator.
  • The most effective process-side lever is LDD (n⁻ extension) — it lengthens l; in this model it's roughly a 700× improvement, at a cost of just a few percent of Idsat.
  • Pocket / halo implants make HCI worse (they shorten l and raise Em), yet they're necessary to control short-channel effects — the textbook dilemma.
  • The most effective circuit-side levers are lowering the drain swing (exponential payoff) and cascode voltage division.
  • The interface itself can also be engineered: nitridation replaces Si–H with Si–N, deuterium anneal replaces H with D — both directly raise φit.

The big picture first: which term does each fix move?

Em ≈ VD − Vdsat l   ,   damage rate ∝ (ID/W) · e−φit / (qλEm) There are only four places to intervene: shrink the numerator (lower VD, raise Vdsat), grow the denominator (increase l), raise φit (swap in a stronger bond), or shorten the exposure time (duty cycle, transition time). Every fix below falls into one of these four categories.

製程層級

製程 · 動分母 l

1. LDD / n⁻ 延伸（Lightly Doped Drain）

在側壁間隔物底下做一段輕摻雜的 n⁻ 延伸區，把重摻雜的 n⁺ 推遠。 汲極電壓的一部分改由這段延伸區的空乏區承擔，高場區被拉長、峰值電場被攤平。 數學上就是 l → l + Wdep,LDD。

代價：n⁻ 區電阻率高 → 串聯電阻 Rext 上升 → Idsat 與 Ron 都變差。 摻雜越輕、延伸越長，HCI 越好但電阻越大。這就是 LDD 劑量調校的全部內容。 另外側壁間隔物寬度直接決定 Lext，所以 spacer 製程的變異會直接反映到 HCI 上。
製程 · 動分母 l（反向）

2. pocket / halo implant —— 治短通道，但惡化 HCI

在源汲接面周圍打入局部的 p 型摻雜（傾角佈植），用來抑制 punchthrough、減少 Vt roll-off 與 DIBL。 但高摻雜讓空乏區縮短，電力線更快被終結 → 有效 l 變小 → Em 上升 → HCI 惡化。

取捨：短通道效應與 HCI 直接對打，沒有兩全解。實務上的折衷： 降低 halo 劑量（犧牲一點 SCE）、只在源極側做 halo（非對稱 halo，汲極側維持低摻雜）、 或調整傾角讓 halo 遠離汲極端的高場點。
用上面的互動可以直接看到這個兩難：打開 halo，Vt 上升（SCE 改善的代價）但損傷率跳好幾十倍。
製程 · 動 φit

3. 界面工程：氮化與氘退火

階段 1 講過，HCI 的永久損傷來自打斷 Si–H 鍵。既然如此，把鍵換強一點就好：

  • 氮化（nitridation）：在界面引入氮，形成鍵能更高的 Si–N 取代部分 Si–H。 同時氮化層也能阻擋硼穿透、提高介電常數。
  • 氘退火（deuterium anneal）：用氘（D）取代氫（H）封懸鍵。D 質量是 H 的兩倍， 振動模式與矽晶格的耦合不同，多聲子激發的解離機率大幅降低。文獻報告可有一個數量級以上的改善。
  • 降低製程誘發損傷：電漿製程的天線效應（antenna）會在閘極累積電荷、先行破壞界面。 遵守 antenna rule、加保護二極體，是「不要在出廠前就先傷一次」。
代價：氮化過量會降低遷移率、增加 NBTI（氮與硼、與 PMOS 的交互作用）。 氘退火需要製程整合與氣體成本，且效果依製程整合方式差異很大。
製程 · 動分子

4. 接面工程：漸變接面與 DDD

比 LDD 更早的做法是 DDD（Double-Diffused Drain）：用兩次不同深度與濃度的佈植做出漸變接面， 讓摻雜濃度從通道到 n⁺ 平滑上升，避免陡峭的接面造成場的尖峰。 現代製程多半以 LDD + spacer 取代，但概念是一樣的：不要讓摻雜濃度突然跳變。

代價：橫向擴散大 → 有效通道長度縮短 → 短通道效應變嚴重、面積效率變差。
製程 · 注意副作用

5. 小心：有些「效能優化」會反過來惡化 HCI

應變矽（strained Si）提高遷移率 µ。但 Esat = 2vsat/µ，µ 上升代表 Esat 下降、 Vdsat 下降 → VD − Vdsat 變大 → Em 上升。 同樣地，減薄 tox 提高驅動能力，但 l = √(3toxxj) 跟著變小，HCI 惡化。

心法：任何讓元件「更快」的手段，幾乎都會讓 HCI 更差。 效能與 HCI 幾乎總是對立的 —— 這不是巧合，兩者都由同一個電場決定。

元件層級

元件 · 動分子與分母

6. 加長通道 L（最直接，但不是最有效）

加長 L 會抬高 Vdsat（因為 EsatL 變大），把分子壓小。 但要記得階段 3 的重點：l 的式子裡沒有 L，所以加長 L 的效果比一般人以為的弱。

代價：面積、電容、速度全部變差，而且 ID ∝ 1/L 直接掉。 實務做法是只對 reliability simulator 標出來的那幾顆熱點元件放大 L，不要全域加長。
元件 · 換一種元件

7. 高壓節點改用厚氧元件

大部分製程都提供 I/O device（厚氧、長通道、高 Vt）。 厚 tox 讓 l 變大、長 L 讓 Vdsat 變大，兩邊都往好的方向走。 凡是要承受接近或超過核心電壓的節點，直接換元件比在核心元件上想辦法有效得多。

代價：速度慢、面積大、Vt 高（低壓下驅動力不足）。
元件 · 高壓專用結構

8. 汲極延伸元件、RESURF、場板

高壓應用（LDMOS、DEMOS）用更徹底的手段把電場攤開： 汲極延伸區（drift region）把高場區拉到很長、 RESURF（降低表面電場）用縱向空乏抵銷橫向電場、 場板（field plate）用金屬或多晶矽電極在表面上方重新塑形電場分布。

代價：面積大幅增加、Ron·A 變差。 高壓元件的設計核心就是 Ron 與崩潰電壓 / HCI 的三角取捨。
元件 · 佈局

9. 非對稱設計與版圖手法

非對稱 LDD：只在汲極側做輕摻雜延伸，源極側維持 n⁺ —— 拿到 HCI 的好處， 又不用付源極串聯電阻（Rs 直接吃 gm）的代價。適用於電流方向固定的元件。
多指佈局：把大 W 拆成多指，降低單指電流密度與自熱。

限制：非對稱元件不能雙向使用，也不能用在源汲會對調的類比開關上。 而且 PDK 未必提供，可能要走客製元件流程。

電路與系統層級

電路 · 動分子（效益最大）

10. 降低汲極擺幅 —— 指數級的效益

損傷率對 Em 是指數敏感，而 Em 直接正比於 VD − Vdsat。 降 10% 的 VD，損傷率往往掉一個數量級。這是所有手段裡投報率最高的，前提是規格允許。

代價：擺幅小 → 訊噪比、動態範圍、驅動能力都變差。 通常是系統架構層級的決定，不是後期能改的。
電路 · 分壓

11. Cascode 疊接

把一顆要承受全電壓的元件，換成兩顆疊接。每顆只承受一半左右的 VDS。 因為損傷率是指數函數，電壓減半的效果遠大於「元件數加倍」的懲罰。 這是類比與 I/O 電路裡最常用的 HCI（與 TDDB）對策。

代價：voltage headroom 少一個 Vdsat、面積增加、需要額外的偏壓電路， 且上下管的偏壓分配要在所有製程角落與暫態下都成立（上電瞬間最容易出事）。
電路 · 動暴露時間

12. 控制轉態時間與避免中間偏壓

階段 5 的工具算過：90% 的損傷集中在週期的 1% 以內，而那個窗口的寬度由 tr/tf 決定。 所以：

  • 邊緣越慢，每次轉態的損傷越多（窗口變寬）。避免不必要的慢 slew。
  • 避免節點長時間停在中間電位：浮接節點、緩慢的上電時序、類比訊號緩慢掃過中間值 —— 這些都會讓元件長時間停在「高 VD + 有電流」的最糟組合。
  • Power gating：不用的區塊直接斷電，等效 duty 直接歸零。
代價：快邊緣意味著更大的瞬時電流、更差的 EMI 與 supply bounce。 這是 HCI 與電源完整性之間的取捨。
方法論

13. Reliability-aware 設計流程

與其猜哪顆元件危險，不如讓工具告訴你。用 foundry PDK 提供的老化模型 （RelXpert / MOSRA / TMI 之類）跑 aging simulation， 沿真實波形積分出每顆元件十年後的退化量，只針對熱點元件補強。

前提：模型要對。foundry 提供的參數有適用範圍， 超出範圍（例如遠低於應力電壓、或極端溫度）的外推，誤差可以是數量級的。 模擬給的是排序，不是保證。

選擇的順序

如果只能做一件事，順序是： ① 降電壓（指數效益，但通常規格綁死）→ ② 換厚氧元件或 cascode（架構層級，早期決定成本最低）→ ③ LDD 劑量調校（製程層級，影響全廠所有產品）→ ④ 針對熱點元件加長 L（後期補救，代價可控）。

越早做，代價越小。等到 qual 才發現 HCI 過不了，能動的只剩最後一項。

檢查你懂了嗎

為什麼 LDD 對 HCI 這麼有效，卻要付串聯電阻的代價？兩者是同一件事嗎？
是同一件事的兩面。LDD 有效是因為 n⁻ 區摻雜低 → 容易被空乏 → 能承擔一部分電壓 → 高場區被拉長。但摻雜低同時代表載子少、電阻率高 —— 這正是串聯電阻的來源。

所以 NLDD 這一個參數同時控制了兩件事，你不可能只要好處。用上面的三檔劑量切切看：往「淡」走，HCI 變好但 Idsat 掉；往「濃」走，反之。LDD 劑量的最佳點就是這條取捨曲線上的一個工程決定。
halo 明明惡化 HCI，為什麼還是每個先進製程都在用？
因為它解決的是更致命的問題。沒有 halo，短通道元件會有嚴重的 punchthrough、Vt roll-off 與 DIBL —— 元件根本關不掉、關態漏電失控、Vt 隨 L 變異劇烈。

相較之下 HCI 是「十年後慢慢退化」，SCE 是「現在就不能用」。可靠度永遠要跟功能競爭，而功能通常贏。可靠度工程師的工作不是消滅 halo，是在 SCE 可接受的前提下把劑量壓到最低、或改用非對稱 halo。
為什麼「加快轉態」可以減少 HCI？直覺上不是電壓變化快比較傷嗎？
因為損傷是對時間積分的。傷害發生在「VD 還很高、通道卻已經開始導通」的那個窗口裡，而窗口的寬度正比於轉態時間 tr。邊緣越慢，元件待在那個窗口裡越久，累積的損傷越多。

電壓變化率本身（dV/dt）不會直接造成傷害 —— HCI 是準靜態的，只看瞬時的 (VG, VD) 組合。

不過要注意：快邊緣會帶來更大的瞬時電流與 EMI，這是另一個層面的代價。
同樣是降低電場，為什麼「降 VD」比「加長 L」有效這麼多？
因為兩者作用在式子的不同位置。降 VD 是直接減少分子 VD−Vdsat；加長 L 只是透過 Vdsat = VgtEsatL/(Vgt+EsatL) 間接抬高 Vdsat，而且這個抬升會飽和（L 很長時 Vdsat → Vgt，再長也沒用）。

更關鍵的是：l 的式子裡沒有 L（階段 3 的重點）。加長 L 完全沒有動到分母。
氘退火為什麼有效？氘和氫化學性質不是幾乎一樣嗎？
化學上幾乎一樣，但質量差兩倍，而 Si–H 的解離走的是多聲子激發路徑 —— 熱載子一次次撞擊，把能量一階一階泵進鍵的振動模式，直到累積超過解離能。

Si–D 的彎曲振動模式頻率與矽晶格的聲子模式接近，激發的能量很快就漏回晶格，不容易一路往上爬。所以同樣的撞擊次數下，D 被打斷的機率低得多。這是一個「動力學」而非「熱力學」的效應 —— 鍵能幾乎一樣，但爬梯子的難度差很多。
身為 foundry，客戶抱怨 HCI 過不了，你的處理順序應該是什麼？
先確認是不是真的問題，再談對策。順序建議：
① 確認判定參數對不對：元件工作在飽和區還是線性區？如果是飽和區用法卻在卡 IDLIN，那是 criterion 選錯，不是元件問題（階段 5）。
② 確認應力條件與使用條件的落差：mission profile 是什麼？外推假設站不站得住？
③ 檢查 halo 劑量：這是 foundry 端最可能有調整空間、又不用改光罩的旋鈕。
④ 建議客戶換元件或改架構：厚氧元件、cascode —— 這通常比改製程快也便宜。
⑤ 最後才是改 LDD / spacer 製程 —— 影響全廠所有產品，代價最高。

Process level

Process · moves the denominator l

1. LDD / n⁻ extension (Lightly Doped Drain)

A lightly-doped n⁻ extension is placed under the sidewall spacer, pushing the heavily-doped n⁺ farther away. Part of the drain voltage is now carried by this extension's depletion region, so the high-field region gets stretched out and the peak field gets flattened. Mathematically it's l → l + Wdep,LDD.

Cost: the n⁻ region's higher resistivity raises the series resistance Rext, hurting both Idsat and Ron. Lighter doping and a longer extension help HCI more but cost more resistance. That trade-off is the entirety of LDD dose tuning. Also, the sidewall spacer width directly sets Lext, so variation in the spacer process shows up directly in HCI.
Process · moves the denominator l (in reverse)

2. Pocket / halo implants — fixes short-channel effects, but worsens HCI

A localized p-type implant (angled implantation) around the source/drain junctions suppresses punchthrough and reduces Vt roll-off and DIBL. But the heavier doping shortens the depletion region, so field lines terminate faster → effective l shrinks → Em rises → HCI worsens.

The trade-off: short-channel effects and HCI pull directly against each other, with no clean solution. Practical compromises: lower the halo dose (sacrificing a bit of SCE control), halo only on the source side (asymmetric halo, keeping the drain side lightly doped), or angle the implant so the halo stays away from the drain's high-field point.
Try the interactive above to see this dilemma directly: turn halo on, and Vt rises (the price of better SCE) while the damage rate jumps by tens of times.
Process · moves φit

3. Interface engineering: nitridation and deuterium anneal

Stage 1 covered how HCI's permanent damage comes from breaking Si–H bonds. So one fix is simply a stronger bond:

  • Nitridation: introduces nitrogen at the interface, forming higher-bond-energy Si–N in place of some Si–H. Nitridation also blocks boron penetration and raises the dielectric constant.
  • Deuterium anneal: uses deuterium (D) instead of hydrogen (H) to cap dangling bonds. D is twice the mass of H, so its vibrational coupling to the silicon lattice is different, and the probability of multi-phonon dissociation drops sharply. The literature reports improvements of an order of magnitude or more.
  • Reducing process-induced damage: the antenna effect in plasma processing can accumulate charge on the gate and damage the interface before the device ever ships. Following antenna rules and adding protection diodes means "don't damage it once before it even leaves the fab."
Cost: over-nitridation lowers mobility and worsens NBTI (nitrogen interacts with boron and with PMOS). Deuterium anneal needs process integration and gas costs, and its effectiveness varies a lot depending on how it's integrated.
Process · moves the numerator

4. Junction engineering: graded junctions and DDD

Predating LDD is DDD (Double-Diffused Drain): two implants at different depths and concentrations create a graded junction, letting doping concentration rise smoothly from channel to n⁺ instead of a sharp junction that spikes the field. Modern processes mostly use LDD + spacer instead, but the concept is the same: don't let doping concentration jump abruptly.

Cost: more lateral diffusion shortens the effective channel length, worsening short-channel effects and area efficiency.
Process · watch for side effects

5. Careful: some "performance optimizations" backfire on HCI

Strained silicon raises mobility µ. But since Esat = 2vsat/µ, a higher µ means a lower Esat, and a lower Vdsat → VD − Vdsat grows → Em rises. Likewise, thinning tox raises drive strength, but l = √(3toxxj) shrinks along with it, worsening HCI.

The mental model: almost any change that makes a device "faster" tends to make HCI worse. Performance and HCI are nearly always in tension — that's not a coincidence, since both are governed by the same field.

Device level

Device · moves both numerator and denominator

6. Lengthen the channel L (most direct, but not the most effective)

Lengthening L raises Vdsat (since EsatL grows), shrinking the numerator. But remember Stage 3's key point: there's no L in the equation for l, so lengthening L helps less than most people assume.

Cost: area, capacitance, and speed all get worse, and ID ∝ 1/L falls directly. In practice, only lengthen L on the specific hotspot devices flagged by a reliability simulator — not globally.
Device · switch device types

7. Use thick-oxide devices at high-voltage nodes

Most processes offer an I/O device (thick oxide, long channel, high Vt). Thick tox grows l, and a long L grows Vdsat — both move in the right direction. For any node that has to withstand near or above core voltage, swapping devices is far more effective than trying to fix a core device.

Cost: slower, larger, and higher Vt (weaker drive at low voltage).
Device · dedicated high-voltage structures

8. Drain-extended devices, RESURF, field plates

High-voltage applications (LDMOS, DEMOS) go further to spread out the field: a drain extension (drift region) stretches the high-field region out over a long distance, RESURF (reduced surface field) uses vertical depletion to offset the lateral field, and field plates use a metal or polysilicon electrode above the surface to reshape the field distribution.

Cost: much larger area and worse Ron·A. The core design problem for high-voltage devices is exactly this three-way trade-off between Ron, breakdown voltage, and HCI.
Device · layout

9. Asymmetric design and layout techniques

Asymmetric LDD: a light-doped extension only on the drain side, keeping n⁺ on the source side — gets HCI's benefit without paying the source-side series-resistance cost (Rs directly eats into gm). Works for devices whose current direction is fixed.
Multi-finger layout: splitting a large W into multiple fingers lowers per-finger current density and self-heating.

Limitation: asymmetric devices can't be used bidirectionally, and can't be used in analog switches where source and drain swap. And a PDK may not offer them, requiring a custom device flow.

Circuit and system level

Circuit · moves the numerator (biggest payoff)

10. Lower the drain swing — an exponential payoff

The damage rate is exponentially sensitive to Em, and Em is directly proportional to VD − Vdsat. A 10% cut in VD often drops the damage rate by an order of magnitude. This has the best ROI of any option, as long as the spec allows it.

Cost: a smaller swing hurts SNR, dynamic range, and drive strength. It's usually a system-architecture-level decision, not something you can fix late.
Circuit · voltage division

11. Cascode stacking

Replace one device that has to withstand the full voltage with two stacked devices, each carrying roughly half the VDS. Because the damage rate is exponential, halving the voltage helps far more than the penalty of doubling the device count. This is the most common HCI (and TDDB) countermeasure in analog and I/O circuits.

Cost: one Vdsat less of voltage headroom, more area, extra bias circuitry needed, and the bias split between the top and bottom devices has to hold across every process corner and transient (power-up is where it's most likely to go wrong).
Circuit · moves the exposure time

12. Control transition time and avoid mid-rail bias

Stage 5's tool showed it: 90% of the damage is packed into 1% of the cycle, and the width of that window is set by tr/tf. So:

  • The slower the edge, the more damage per transition (the window widens). Avoid unnecessarily slow slew.
  • Avoid leaving a node sitting at mid-rail for a long time: a floating node, a slow power-up sequence, an analog signal that crawls slowly through mid-swing — all of these leave the device sitting in the worst combination, "high VD plus current flowing," for a long time.
  • Power gating: cutting power to unused blocks sends the effective duty cycle straight to zero.
Cost: faster edges mean larger instantaneous currents, worse EMI, and more supply bounce. This is a trade-off between HCI and power integrity.
Methodology

13. Reliability-aware design flow

Instead of guessing which device is at risk, let a tool tell you. Use the foundry PDK's aging models (things like RelXpert / MOSRA / TMI) to run an aging simulation, integrating each device's ten-year degradation along its real waveform, and reinforce only the hotspot devices.

Precondition: the model has to be right. Foundry-supplied parameters have a valid range, and extrapolating outside it (e.g. far below the stress voltage, or extreme temperatures) can introduce order-of-magnitude errors. Simulation gives you a ranking, not a guarantee.

Priority order

If you can only do one thing, the order is: ① Lower the voltage (exponential payoff, but usually spec-locked) → ② Switch to a thick-oxide device or add cascode (architecture-level, cheapest when decided early) → ③ Tune the LDD dose (process-level, affects every product in the fab) → ④ Lengthen L on the hotspot devices (a late-stage fix, but with controllable cost).

The earlier you act, the cheaper it is. Discover an HCI failure at qual, and the only lever left is the last one.

Check your understanding

Why is LDD so effective for HCI, yet costs series resistance? Are the two the same thing?
Two sides of the same coin. LDD works because the n⁻ region's low doping is easily depleted → it can absorb part of the voltage → the high-field region gets stretched out. But that same low doping also means fewer carriers and higher resistivity — exactly the source of series resistance.

So the single parameter NLDD controls both things at once — you can't have the benefit without the cost. Try the three dose levels above: go "light" and HCI improves but Idsat falls; go "heavy" and it's the reverse. The optimal LDD dose is an engineering decision on this one trade-off curve.
Halo implants clearly make HCI worse — so why does every advanced process still use them?
Because they solve a more fatal problem. Without halo, short-channel devices suffer severe punchthrough, Vt roll-off, and DIBL — the device can't turn off properly, off-state leakage runs away, and Vt varies wildly with L.

By comparison, HCI is "slow degradation over ten years," while SCE is "doesn't work right now." Reliability always competes with functionality, and functionality usually wins. A reliability engineer's job isn't to eliminate halo — it's to push the dose as low as SCE allows, or switch to an asymmetric halo.
Why does "speeding up transitions" reduce HCI? Intuitively, shouldn't a faster voltage change be more damaging?
Because the damage is integrated over time. The damage happens in the window where "VD is still high but the channel has already started conducting," and that window's width is proportional to the transition time tr. The slower the edge, the longer the device sits in that window, and the more damage accumulates.

The rate of voltage change itself (dV/dt) doesn't directly cause damage — HCI is quasi-static and only depends on the instantaneous (VG, VD) pair.

One caveat: fast edges bring larger instantaneous currents and worse EMI — a cost at a different level.
Both lower the field, so why is "lowering VD" so much more effective than "lengthening L"?
Because they act on different parts of the equation. Lowering VD directly shrinks the numerator VD−Vdsat; lengthening L only indirectly raises Vdsat, through Vdsat = VgtEsatL/(Vgt+EsatL), and that rise saturates (for a long L, Vdsat → Vgt, and going longer stops helping).

More critically: there's no L in the equation for l (Stage 3's key point). Lengthening L doesn't touch the denominator at all.
Why does deuterium anneal work? Aren't deuterium and hydrogen chemically almost identical?
Chemically, almost identical — but they differ 2× in mass, and Si–H dissociation proceeds via multi-phonon excitation — repeated hits from hot carriers pump energy step by step into the bond's vibrational mode until it exceeds the dissociation energy.

Si–D's bending-mode frequency sits closer to the silicon lattice's phonon modes, so the excitation energy leaks back into the lattice quickly, making it hard to climb the ladder all the way up. So for the same number of hits, D is far less likely to break loose. This is a "kinetic," not a "thermodynamic," effect — the bond energy is almost the same, but the difficulty of climbing the ladder is very different.
As a foundry, a customer complains HCI doesn't pass. What order should you work through this in?
First confirm it's a real problem, then talk countermeasures. Suggested order:
① Confirm the criterion is the right one: does the device operate in saturation or linear region? If it's used in saturation but is failing on IDLIN, that's the wrong criterion, not a device problem (Stage 5).
② Confirm the gap between stress condition and use condition: what's the mission profile? Do the extrapolation assumptions hold up?
③ Check the halo dose: this is the knob most likely to have room to adjust on the foundry side without changing the reticle.
④ Suggest the customer switch devices or change architecture: thick-oxide devices, cascode — usually faster and cheaper than a process change.
⑤ Only as a last resort, change the LDD / spacer process — that affects every product in the fab, and is the most expensive option.
