介電層時效崩潰（TDDB）
從滲流直覺到壽命外推

同一顆閘極氧化層，即使電壓從沒超過規格上限，用久了還是可能突然短路——這就是 TDDB（Time-Dependent Dielectric Breakdown，介電層時效崩潰）。 它跟 HCI 那種「參數慢慢漂移」完全不同：崩潰前幾乎沒有預兆，一崩潰就是硬短路。 這份教材把它拆成七個階段：先靠一個比喻建立直覺，再看真正在跑的滲流模擬，然後是統計與外推、業界規格，最後給你一個可以隨便調的壽命模型玩到懂為止。 每一頁都短，一次只講一件事，卡住了隨時可以往回翻。

30 秒版本

把氧化層想成一片薄薄的絕緣磚牆，電子每次穿隧通過，都有一個很小的機率在牆裡留下一個「壞掉的磚塊」（缺陷）。 壞磚塊會隨機出現、越積越多，大部分時候彼此不相連，牆仍然擋得住電流。

但只要有一串壞磚塊剛好從閘極一路連到矽基板，電流就找到了一條現成的捷徑，瞬間貫穿——這就是崩潰的瞬間。 這個「隨機累積、直到連成一條路」的過程叫滲流（percolation），整個 TDDB 教材幾乎都在講這件事的後果： 既然哪一顆先連通是隨機的，那麼同一批、同樣製程、同樣應力條件的元件，壞掉的時間點就會散開成一個統計分布， 而不是所有元件在同一秒一起壞掉。

HCI

連續漂移

參數一點一點變差，工程師人為畫一條線（例如漂移 10%、Vt 偏移 50 mV）宣告失效。 每顆元件的退化曲線幾乎重疊，統計離散度不是重點，物理機制才是。

TDDB

一次性崩潰

元件在崩潰前幾乎沒有徵兆，然後突然短路，沒有中間地帶。 同一批元件壞掉的時間差很多，統計分布本身就是重點——你要保證的是「十年後、百萬顆裡只有一顆壞」，而不是「平均多久會壞」。

Time-Dependent Dielectric Breakdown (TDDB)
from percolation intuition to lifetime extrapolation

Even a gate oxide that has never once been driven above its rated voltage can still suddenly short out after enough time in service — that's TDDB (Time-Dependent Dielectric Breakdown). It's fundamentally different from HCI's slow parameter drift: there's almost no warning before breakdown, and once it happens, it's a hard short. This course breaks it into seven stages: start with an analogy to build intuition, watch a real percolation simulation run in front of you, then move through statistics and extrapolation, industry specs, and finish with a fully tunable lifetime model to play with until it clicks. Every page is short and covers exactly one idea — you can always flip back.

The 30-second version

Think of the oxide as a thin brick wall of insulation. Every time an electron tunnels through, there's a small chance it leaves behind one "broken brick" (a defect) somewhere in the wall. Broken bricks appear randomly and pile up over time; most of the time they're scattered and disconnected, so the wall still blocks current.

But the instant a chain of broken bricks happens to connect all the way from the gate to the silicon substrate, current finds a ready-made shortcut straight through — that instant is breakdown. This "random accumulation until a path connects" process is called percolation, and nearly this entire course is about the consequences of that one fact: since which device connects first is random, an entire batch of identical devices under identical stress will fail at scattered times that form a statistical distribution — not all at the same instant.

HCI

Continuous drift

A parameter degrades bit by bit, and engineers draw an arbitrary line (e.g. 10% drift, or a 50 mV Vt shift) and call that "failed." Every device's degradation curve nearly overlaps the others — statistical spread isn't the point here; the physical mechanism is.

TDDB

One-shot breakdown

A device shows almost no warning signs, then suddenly shorts — there's no in-between state. Failure times within the same batch scatter widely, so the statistical distribution itself is the whole point — what you have to guarantee is "only one in a million fails after ten years," not "the average time to fail."
