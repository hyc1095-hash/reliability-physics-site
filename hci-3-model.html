階段 3 / 7 · 物理模型

把「電場很強」變成可以算的東西

前兩頁都在說「汲極端電場很強」。這一頁要回答：到底多強？ 只有算得出數字，才能預測壽命、比較製程、決定規格。

一分鐘版
  • 不能用「電壓 ÷ 通道長度」—— 電場極不均勻，那樣算會低估好幾倍。
  • 正確的做法叫 pseudo-2D 模型：解出 E(y) = Esat·cosh(y/l) 這個形狀。
  • 裡面的 l = √(3·tox·xj) 是特徵長度 —— 它不含 L，這一點常被誤解。
  • 溫度只從一個地方進來：平均自由程 λ(T)。這就是活化能為負的全部原因。

第一個問題：為什麼不能用平均電場？

最直覺的算法是「電壓除以距離」：E = VD/L。對一條均勻的電阻絲，這是對的。 但夾止之後的 MOSFET 通道完全不均勻 —— 通道大部分區域的電位緩慢上升， 所有多出來的電壓幾乎全部落在汲極端那一小段上。

Stage 3 / 7 · Physical Model

Turning "the field is strong" into something computable

The last two pages both said "the field near the drain is strong." This page answers: how strong, exactly? Only once you can compute a number can you predict lifetime, compare processes, or set a spec.

The 1-minute version
  • You can't use "voltage ÷ channel length" — the field is extremely non-uniform, and that would underestimate it by several times.
  • The correct approach is called a pseudo-2D model: solve for the shape E(y) = Esat·cosh(y/l).
  • The l = √(3·tox·xj) inside it is a characteristic length — it contains no L, a point that's commonly misunderstood.
  • Temperature enters through exactly one place: the mean free path λ(T). That's the entire reason the activation energy is negative.

Question one: why can't you use the average field?

The most intuitive approach is "voltage divided by distance": E = VD/L. For a uniform resistive wire, that's correct. But a pinched-off MOSFET channel is completely non-uniform — the potential rises slowly across most of the channel, and almost all of the extra voltage drops across that one short stretch near the drain.

第二個問題：那一小段有多長？

這是 pseudo-2D 模型的核心。想法是：取通道裡的一個薄片，對它用高斯定律。 電力線不只沿著通道走，還會往上漏到閘極、往下漏到基板。 把這件事寫成方程式，會得到一個帶有特徵長度 l 的二階微分方程，解出來就是 cosh 的形狀。

特徵長度 l = √( εSi/εox · tox · xj ) ≈ √( 3 · tox · xj ) 物理意義：電力線「漏」出通道的尺度。氧化層越薄，閘極把電力線抓得越緊、漏得越少 → l 越小； 接面越淺，可供電力線散開的縱深越少 → l 也越小。l 越小，同樣的電壓被擠進越短的距離，電場就越強。

Question two: how long is that one short stretch?

This is the core of the pseudo-2D model. The idea: take a thin slice of the channel and apply Gauss's law to it. Field lines don't just run along the channel — they also leak upward into the gate and downward into the substrate. Writing that as an equation gives a second-order differential equation with a characteristic length l, whose solution is the cosh shape.

Characteristic length l = √( εSi/εox · tox · xj ) ≈ √( 3 · tox · xj ) Physical meaning: the length scale over which field lines "leak" out of the channel. A thinner oxide means the gate holds field lines tighter, so less leaks → smaller l; a shallower junction leaves less depth for field lines to spread into → l also shrinks. The smaller l is, the same voltage gets squeezed into a shorter distance, and the field gets stronger.

最容易誤解的一點

拖 L 的滑桿，再拖 tox 的滑桿，比較 Em 的變化幅度。

l 的式子裡根本沒有 L。單獨縮短通道長度，只能透過 Vdsat 下降來間接抬升 Em，效果有限。 真正讓每一個縮放世代 HCI 惡化的，是 tox 與 xj 跟著一起縮小 —— 同樣的壓降被擠進更短的距離。 「短通道元件 HCI 比較嚴重」是對的，但原因不是「短」，是「薄」和「淺」。

第三個問題：溫度從哪裡進來？

整個模型裡，溫度只影響一個量：熱載子的平均自由程 λ。 溫度越高，晶格振動（聲子）越劇烈，電子越容易被撞散，λ 就越短。

平均自由程的溫度依賴 λ(T) = λ0 · tanh( Ep / 2kT ) Ep ≈ 0.063 eV 是矽的光學聲子能量，λ0 ≈ 10.4 nm 是絕對零度的極限值。 高溫時 tanh 的引數變小，λ 跟著變小 —— 電子拿不到那麼多能量。
The point most people get wrong

Drag the L slider, then the tox slider, and compare how much each moves Em.

The equation for l contains no L at all. Shortening the channel length by itself only raises Em indirectly, through a drop in Vdsat, and that effect is limited. What actually makes HCI worse with every scaling generation is tox and xj shrinking together — the same voltage drop gets squeezed into a shorter distance. "Short-channel devices suffer worse HCI" is true, but the reason isn't "short" — it's "thin" and "shallow."

Question three: where does temperature come in?

Across the whole model, temperature affects exactly one quantity: the hot carrier's mean free path λ. The higher the temperature, the more violently the lattice vibrates (phonons), the more easily electrons get scattered, and the shorter λ becomes.

Temperature dependence of the mean free path λ(T) = λ0 · tanh( Ep / 2kT ) Ep ≈ 0.063 eV is silicon's optical phonon energy, and λ0 ≈ 10.4 nm is the limiting value at absolute zero. At high temperature, tanh's argument shrinks, and λ shrinks with it — electrons simply don't get to keep as much energy.

檢查你懂了嗎

為什麼 l 的式子裡有 tox 和 xj，卻沒有 L？
因為 l 描述的是電力線往垂直方向漏出去的尺度，這件事只跟「上面的閘極有多近（tox）」和「下面有多少縱深可以散開（xj）」有關，跟通道有多長無關。

可以這樣想：l 決定了汲極的電位擾動能往源極方向「傳」多遠。這是一個局部的幾何性質。
如果我把 tox 減半，Em 大概變成幾倍？
l ∝ √tox，所以 tox 減半 → l 變成 1/√2 ≈ 0.71 倍 → Em ≈ (VD−Vdsat)/l 變成約 1.41 倍。

聽起來不多？但損傷率是 e−B/Em。以典型的 B/Em ≈ 12 來算，Em 變 1.41 倍會讓指數從 −12 變成 −8.5，損傷率跳約 e3.5 ≈ 33 倍。這就是為什麼薄氧化層的 HCI 這麼難搞。
為什麼 VG 拉高反而可能讓 Em 下降？
因為 Vdsat 隨 VG 上升。夾止區的壓降是 VD − Vdsat，Vdsat 變大就代表落在高場區的電壓變少 → Em 下降。

但另一方面 VG 上升會讓 ID 增加。兩個效應打架，就生出了 Isub 的鐘形曲線，峰值大約落在 VG ≈ VD/2。階段 5 可以親眼看到這條曲線自己長出來。
λ(T) 用 tanh 而不是簡單的指數，有什麼道理？
tanh(Ep/2kT) 來自聲子的佔據數（Bose–Einstein 分布）。低溫時聲子很少，λ 趨近極限值 λ0；高溫時聲子數 ∝ T，散射率 ∝ T，λ ∝ 1/T。tanh 剛好平滑地連接這兩個極限。

這叫 Crowell–Sze 修正，是處理熱載子輸運的標準做法。

Check your understanding

Why does the equation for l contain tox and xj, but no L?
Because l describes the length scale over which field lines leak in the vertical direction, which only depends on "how close the gate is above (tox)" and "how much depth is available to spread into below (xj)" — nothing to do with how long the channel is.

Think of it this way: l determines how far the drain's potential disturbance can "reach" back toward the source. It's a local geometric property.
If I halve tox, roughly how many times bigger does Em get?
l ∝ √tox, so halving tox → l becomes 1/√2 ≈ 0.71× → Em ≈ (VD−Vdsat)/l becomes roughly 1.41×.

Doesn't sound like much? But the damage rate goes as e−B/Em. With a typical B/Em ≈ 12, a 1.41× change in Em shifts the exponent from −12 to −8.5, jumping the damage rate by roughly e3.5 ≈ 33×. That's why HCI is so hard to manage in thin-oxide devices.
Why can raising VG actually lower Em?
Because Vdsat rises with VG. The pinch-off region's voltage drop is VD − Vdsat, so a bigger Vdsat means less voltage lands in the high-field region → Em falls.

But on the other hand, raising VG increases ID. Those two effects fighting each other is exactly what produces Isub's bell-shaped curve, peaking roughly at VG ≈ VD/2. Stage 5 lets you watch that curve grow out of the model yourself.
Why tanh instead of a simple exponential for λ(T)?
tanh(Ep/2kT) comes from the phonon occupation number (a Bose–Einstein distribution). At low temperature, phonons are scarce and λ approaches its limiting value λ0; at high temperature, the phonon count ∝ T, the scattering rate ∝ T, and λ ∝ 1/T. tanh smoothly connects these two limits.

This is called the Crowell–Sze correction, the standard treatment for hot-carrier transport.
