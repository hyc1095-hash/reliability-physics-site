靜態偏壓，不是電流

NBTI 最容易搞混的地方，是拿 HCI 的直覺去套：以為要有電流流動才會有可靠度問題。 NBTI 完全相反 —— 它只需要一個負閘壓把通道「反轉住」，不需要任何汲極電流。

一分鐘版

pMOS 閘極加負偏壓、源汲極都接地（VD=VS=0）就能產生 NBTI 應力。 真正驅動劣化的參數是通道表面的電洞反轉層濃度 pinv，一個純粹垂直方向的靜態量， 跟橫向電場、電流密度完全無關。

跟 HCI 對照，一次看懂差異

關鍵直覺

HCI 是「跑得快才會受傷」的機制；NBTI 是「站著不動也會受傷」的機制。 一顆待機中的 pMOS（閘極維持負偏壓、沒有任何開關動作）在 HCI 角度看是完全安全的， 在 NBTI 角度看卻正在慢慢劣化。

檢查你懂了嗎

為什麼 nMOS 也會有類似現象（PBTI），但機制不完全一樣？
PBTI 發生在 nMOS 正閘壓、電子反轉層累積時。但 PBTI 的主要機制較常被歸因於電子穿隧進閘極介電層裡的既有陷阱 （trap-up），跟 NBTI 的 Si-H 鍵斷裂生成新界面態不完全一樣。這也是為什麼本教材先把 pMOS 的 NBTI 講完整， PBTI 留待日後獨立展開，而不是硬把兩者當同一個機制處理。
一顆電路裡，哪些電晶體最容易受 NBTI 影響？
閘極長時間維持在固定負偏壓的 pMOS 最危險 —— 例如靜態邏輯閘裡長時間輸出低電位、 輸入端固定不切換的 pMOS，或類比電路裡偏壓固定的電流鏡 pMOS。反而是頻繁開關的 pMOS （每次切換都有機會落在恢復窗口），淨劣化量可能比想像中小 —— 這跟階段 3 要談的恢復動力學直接相關。

Stage 1 / 4 · Intuition

Static bias, not current

The easiest mistake with NBTI is applying HCI intuition to it: assuming current has to be flowing for there to be a reliability problem. NBTI is the exact opposite — all it needs is a negative gate bias to "invert" the channel; no drain current is required at all.

The 1-minute version

Applying a negative bias to a pMOS gate with source and drain both grounded (VD=VS=0) is already enough to produce NBTI stress. The parameter that actually drives the degradation is the hole-inversion-layer concentration at the channel surface, pinv — a purely vertical, static quantity, completely unrelated to lateral field or current density.

Side by side with HCI: see the differences at once

AspectHCINBTI
Needs current?Yes — channel current is a prerequisite for impact ionizationNo — happens even at VD=VS=0
Driving parameterLateral field Em, current densityVertical field, pinv (hole-inversion-layer concentration)

Direction of temperature activationNegative (worse at low temperature)Positive (worse at high temperature)

The key intuition

HCI is a mechanism where "running fast is what hurts you"; NBTI is a mechanism where "just standing still hurts you too." An idle pMOS (gate held at a fixed negative bias, no switching activity at all) is perfectly safe from HCI's point of view, but from NBTI's point of view, it's slowly degrading the whole time.

Check your understanding

Why does nMOS show a similar phenomenon (PBTI), but with a mechanism that isn't quite the same?
PBTI happens on nMOS under positive gate bias, as an electron inversion layer accumulates. But PBTI's dominant mechanism is more often attributed to electrons tunneling into pre-existing traps in the gate dielectric (trap-up), which isn't quite the same as NBTI's Si-H bond breaking that generates new interface traps. That's also why this course covers pMOS NBTI fully first, leaving PBTI for a separate future treatment, rather than forcing the two into the same mechanism.
In a circuit, which transistors are most vulnerable to NBTI?
A pMOS whose gate sits at a fixed negative bias for long stretches is the most at risk — for example, a pMOS in a static logic gate that outputs low for long periods with its input never switching, or a current-mirror pMOS in an analog circuit held at a fixed bias. Ironically, a pMOS that switches frequently (each switch is a chance to land in the recovery window) may end up with less net degradation than you'd expect — which connects directly to the recovery dynamics covered in Stage 3.
