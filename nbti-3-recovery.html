階段 3 / 4 · 恢復動力學與量測陷阱

移除應力的瞬間，訊號已經開始回復

這是 NBTI 最麻煩的地方：一部分界面態會被鈍化回復、一部分被侷限的電荷會被釋放， ΔVt 在對數時間尺度上快速回彈，接著趨緩。傳統量測方法會系統性低估真實劣化量。

為什麼傳統量測會踩到這個陷阱

傳統做法是「先移除應力偏壓、再切到量測模式量 I-V」。這個切換本身需要時間 —— 通常毫秒到秒級 —— 而這段時間已經足以讓恢復吃掉可觀的訊號量。應力時間越短，這個誤差佔的比例越大， 因為短時間應力點量到的 ΔVt 本來就小，恢復掉的那一小塊相對影響更劇烈。

關鍵直覺

你以為量到的是「劣化量」，量到的其實是「劣化量 − 量測延遲期間已經回復掉的量」。 這不是雜訊，是系統性偏誤，而且偏誤大小隨量測方法本身變化，不是元件的物理性質。

業界怎麼解決：OTF 與 ultra-fast I-V

方法做法解決了什麼
On-the-fly（OTF）不中斷應力，直接在應力偏壓下即時量測（利用應力偏壓本身推算 Vt 變化） 完全避開「移除應力→切換→量測」這段恢復窗口
Ultra-fast I-V把量測本身壓縮到微秒級，仍然是移除應力後量測，但盡量縮短恢復發生的時間窗 大幅縮小（但不是完全消除）恢復造成的低估

檢查你懂了嗎

為什麼「應力時間越短，量測陷阱誤差比例越大」？
恢復掉的訊號量在早期應力階段跟總劣化量相比，佔的比例通常比後期更高 （恢復動力學本身也接近對數時間律，跟劣化的冪次律時間常數不同步）。 早期應力點的 ΔVt 絕對值本來就小，同樣的恢復量佔的相對比例就被放大， 使得早期數據點看起來比真實情況「乾淨」很多、n 值擬合結果因此被扭曲。
如果不管恢復陷阱，直接用傳統量測的數據去外推 10 年壽命，會高估還是低估風險？
低估風險（過度樂觀）。因為傳統量測系統性低估了真實 ΔVt， 外推出來的失效時間會比實際更長、允許的判準門檻在等效意義上被放寬了。 這跟階段 2 提到「不要把理論 n 值當通用常數」是同一個工程教訓的不同面向： 模型參數的準確性，前提是量測方法本身要先站得住腳。

Stage 3 / 4 · Recovery Dynamics & Measurement Traps

The instant stress is removed, the signal has already started recovering

This is NBTI's trickiest aspect: some interface traps get passivated and recover, and some trapped charge gets released, so ΔVt snaps back quickly on a logarithmic time scale and then levels off. Conventional measurement methods systematically underestimate the true degradation.

Why conventional measurement falls into this trap

The conventional approach is "remove the stress bias first, then switch to measurement mode to measure I-V." That switch itself takes time — typically milliseconds to seconds — and that's already enough time for recovery to eat up a significant amount of signal. The shorter the stress time, the larger this error's proportional impact, because the ΔVt measured at an early stress point is already small, so the same amount recovered has a proportionally bigger effect.

The key intuition

You think you're measuring "the amount of degradation," but you're actually measuring "the amount of degradation minus whatever already recovered during the measurement delay." This isn't noise — it's a systematic bias, and the size of the bias changes with the measurement method itself, not with the device's physics.

How industry deals with it: OTF and ultra-fast I-V

MethodApproachWhat it fixes
On-the-fly (OTF)Never interrupts the stress — measures live under the stress bias itself (using the stress bias itself to infer the Vt change) Completely avoids the "remove stress → switch → measure" recovery window
Ultra-fast I-VCompresses the measurement itself down to microseconds — still measures after removing stress, but minimizes the time window in which recovery can happen Substantially shrinks (but doesn't fully eliminate) the underestimate caused by recovery

Check your understanding

Why does "the shorter the stress time, the bigger the measurement-trap error's proportional impact"?
The amount of signal recovered, compared to the total degradation, is usually a bigger fraction early in the stress than it is later (recovery dynamics themselves are close to a logarithmic time law, out of step with degradation's power-law time constant). The absolute ΔVt at an early stress point is already small, so the same amount of recovery represents a proportionally larger fraction — making early data points look far "cleaner" than reality, which distorts the fitted n value.
If you ignore the recovery trap and extrapolate a 10-year lifetime directly from conventionally measured data, does that overestimate or underestimate the risk?
It underestimates the risk (overly optimistic). Because conventional measurement systematically underestimates the true ΔVt, the extrapolated failure time comes out longer than it really is, and the allowed pass/fail threshold is effectively loosened. This is a different facet of the same engineering lesson Stage 2 raised — "don't treat the theoretical n value as a universal constant": a model parameter's accuracy is only as good as the measurement method it was extracted with.
