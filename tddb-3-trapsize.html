Stage 3 / 7

Trap size and the Weibull slope:
β hides two parameters you never saw

Stage 2 produced a beautiful result — β ≈ tox/a₀ — and we set a₀ to 1 nm. But where did that 1 nm come from? Open the literature and you find something uncomfortable: fitting the same percolation model to breakdown distributions, different papers extract trap sizes a₀ anywhere from 0.4 nm to 3 nm — nearly an order of magnitude apart. And since β is inversely proportional to a₀, that one disagreement alone can shift the ppm lifetime extrapolated from the same data by orders of magnitude. This page opens a₀ up: what size it actually is, why every paper measures a different one, and what that costs you in a spec.

① β 不是一個數，是兩個數的乘積

階段 2 用「缺陷一顆顆隨機長出來，連成一條路就崩潰」導出 Weibull 分布，形狀參數等於路徑上的缺陷數 N。 那個推導其實偷偷用了一個假設：缺陷密度隨時間線性增加。 真實的陷阱生成不是線性的，而是冪次律：Ntrap ∝ tm。把這個 m 放回去，關係式變成：

cell-based 滲流模型的 Weibull 斜率 β = m · N = m · tox / a₀ β 是時間分布的形狀參數，而滲流幾何只決定缺陷密度分布的形狀（那個才等於 N）。 兩者之間差的就是 m —— 把「要幾顆缺陷」翻譯成「要多久」的匯率。

m—陷阱生成冪次，Ntrap ∝ tm。實測約 0.26–1.0
N—一條貫穿路徑所需的缺陷數 = tox/a₀
a₀nm陷阱尺寸，也就是滲流格點的邊長
階段 2 偷偷做了什麼
階段 2 的蒙地卡羅假設缺陷密度隨時間線性增加，等於設定 m = 1。 所以那一頁量到的 β 其實就是 N，看起來 β = tox/a₀ 乾淨得不像話。 一旦 m 回到實測的 0.26–0.6，同樣的 tox 與 a₀ 會給出只有一半甚至四分之一的 β。 這也是為什麼先進製程量到的 β 常常低於 1 —— 不一定是製程不好，而是 m 與 a₀ 兩個因子一起把它壓下來。

這條關係式的原始出處是 Suñé 在 2001 年提出的解析 cell-based 模型。 它的賣點正是「不用跑蒙地卡羅也能得到正確的厚度縮放」：摘要原文寫的是 “The scaling with oxide thickness of the Weibull slope and the mean critical density of defects at breakdown are accounted for correctly.”

同一條式子在高介電常數材料的文獻裡常寫成 β = m·βot，其中 βot 就是路徑上的陷阱數。 O'Connor 等人 2010 年的 IRPS 論文把它的意涵講得最直白： “a high Weibull slope is caused either by more traps being required to trigger the breakdown or a higher trap generation rate.” —— β 高只有兩種可能：路徑需要更多顆陷阱（a₀ 小），或者陷阱生成得更快（m 大）。單看 β 分不出是哪一個。

① β isn't one number, it's a product of two

Stage 2 derived the Weibull distribution from "defects appear one at a time at random, and a connected path means breakdown," with the shape parameter equal to the number of defects N in the path. That derivation quietly used one assumption: defect density grows linearly with time. Real trap generation isn't linear — it's a power law: Ntrap ∝ tm. Put that m back in and the relation becomes:

Weibull slope in the cell-based percolation model β = m · N = m · tox / a₀ β is the shape parameter of the time distribution, while percolation geometry only sets the shape of the defect-density distribution (that one equals N). What sits between them is m — the exchange rate that converts "how many defects" into "how long."
β—Weibull shape parameter (slope), for the time distribution
m—trap-generation power-law exponent, Ntrap ∝ tm. Measured values ≈ 0.26–1.0
N—number of defects needed for a percolating path = tox/a₀
a₀nmtrap size — equivalently, the side length of the percolation cell
What Stage 2 quietly did
Stage 2's Monte Carlo assumed defect density grows linearly with time, which is the same as setting m = 1. So the β measured on that page really was N, which is why β = tox/a₀ came out implausibly clean. Restore m to a measured 0.26–0.6 and the same tox and a₀ give a β half or even a quarter as large. That is also why advanced processes routinely measure β below 1 — not necessarily a bad process, but m and a₀ pushing it down together.

The relation originates in the analytic cell-based model Suñé published in 2001. Its whole selling point is getting the thickness scaling right without running a Monte Carlo; the abstract reads “The scaling with oxide thickness of the Weibull slope and the mean critical density of defects at breakdown are accounted for correctly.”

The same equation is usually written β = m·βot in the high-k literature, where βot is the number of traps in the path. O'Connor et al.'s 2010 IRPS paper states the implication most bluntly: “a high Weibull slope is caused either by more traps being required to trigger the breakdown or a higher trap generation rate.” — there are only two ways to get a high β: the path needs more traps (small a₀), or traps are generated faster (large m). From β alone you cannot tell which.

② a₀ 到底是什麼尺寸

最容易犯的錯是把 a₀ 當成「一個氧空缺有多大」。它不是。 a₀ 是一個缺陷在電性上能搭上下一個缺陷的距離 —— 決定它的是捕獲截面與陷阱輔助穿隧的重疊範圍，不是原子半徑。 所以同樣的物理缺陷，換一種介電質、換一個陷阱能階，a₀ 就會變。

這就是「陷阱尺寸會改變 Weibull 分布」的物理槓桿
陷阱能階越淺、位能障越低 → 捕獲截面越大 → a₀ 越大 → 一條貫穿路徑需要的缺陷數 N 越少 → β 越小、分布越散。 反過來，深層陷阱的捕獲截面小，a₀ 小，同樣厚度需要更多顆缺陷才連得起來，β 就高。

high-k 材料正是這個效應的典型案例。O'Connor 等人的說法是： “it is commonly accepted that due the reduced barrier height of the high-k compared with SiO2 the effective capture cross section of a high-k defect is larger than that of an SiO2 defect, meaning that the percolation distance is more likely to be reduced in the high-k.” 翻成人話：high-k 的缺陷「比較大顆」，所以兩三顆就能把路連起來，而同樣厚度的 SiO₂ 需要三顆以上。

而這個「缺陷有尺寸」的觀點本身也有極限。Suñé、Wu 與 Tous 在 2007 年直接點名這件事： “this simple geometric picture reaches its limit when the oxide thickness is scaled down and becomes comparable to the ‘size’ of the involved defects.” 他們的做法是把純幾何的格點模型換掉，改用陷阱輔助穿隧耦合缺陷位置的統計分布， 於是分布形狀會隨你用多大的漏電跳階當崩潰判準（IBD）而改變 —— a₀ 不再是一個固定長度，而是一個跟量測條件綁在一起的量。

② What size a₀ actually is

The easiest mistake is to read a₀ as "how big one oxygen vacancy is." It isn't. a₀ is the distance over which one defect can electrically reach the next — set by the capture cross section and the overlap range for trap-assisted tunneling, not by an atomic radius. So the same physical defect gives a different a₀ in a different dielectric, or at a different trap energy level.

This is the physical lever behind "trap size changes the Weibull distribution"
Shallower trap level, lower barrier → larger capture cross section → larger a₀ → fewer defects N needed for a percolating path → smaller β, wider distribution. Conversely, deep traps have a small capture cross section and thus a small a₀, so the same thickness needs more defects to connect, and β comes out high.

High-k dielectrics are the textbook case. O'Connor et al. put it this way: “it is commonly accepted that due the reduced barrier height of the high-k compared with SiO2 the effective capture cross section of a high-k defect is larger than that of an SiO2 defect, meaning that the percolation distance is more likely to be reduced in the high-k.” In plain terms: high-k defects are "bigger," so two or three of them bridge the layer, where the same thickness of SiO₂ needs three or more.

The "defects have a size" picture has its own limit, and Suñé, Wu and Tous named it directly in 2007: “this simple geometric picture reaches its limit when the oxide thickness is scaled down and becomes comparable to the ‘size’ of the involved defects.” Their fix was to drop the purely geometric lattice and couple a trap-assisted-tunneling model to the statistics of defect positions, so the distribution shape then depends on how large a leakage jump you call breakdown (IBD) — a₀ stops being a fixed length and becomes a quantity tied to your measurement condition.

為什麼這六個數字不互相矛盾
因為 a₀ 不是一個可以獨立量測的物理長度，而是某個特定滲流幾何的擬合參數。 球體重疊模型的「半徑」、立方格點的「有效缺陷尺寸」、cell 模型的「格子邊長」三者定義都不同； 再加上 β = m·tox/a₀ 裡 m 與 a₀ 乘在一起，只量 β 對 tox 的斜率，本質上無法把 m 和 a₀ 分開。 所以引用任何一個 a₀ 數值時，必須同時引用它搭配的幾何與 m，否則數字沒有意義。
一個具體的工程案例：用 β = m·N 反推，結論會隨 a₀ 假設翻轉

O'Connor 等人在 8 Å EOT 的 HfO₂ nMOSFET 上量到 β = 1.54（7.8 Å）與 1.82（8.1 Å）， 遠高於同厚度文獻常見的 0.8–1。他們的推論鏈是：

  1. 若沿用 SiO₂ 的滲流距離，這個厚度需要 N = 3 顆陷阱 → 由 β = 1.82 回推 m = 0.61，比文獻常見的 0.35–0.4 高很多。
  2. 但 high-k 的捕獲截面比 SiO₂ 大，滲流距離應該更短（a₀ 更大）→ N 應該是 2，或 3 顆但排列不良。
  3. N 若降到 2，回推的 m 還要更高。兩條路都指向「這個缺陷的生成速率真的異常高」，而不是路徑特別長。
注意第 2 步做了什麼：只是改了陷阱尺寸的假設，對 m 的結論就被往上推。 同一個 β，換個 a₀ 假設，你會把它歸因到完全不同的物理。

Why these six numbers don't contradict each other
Because a₀ is not an independently measurable physical length — it's a fit parameter of one particular percolation geometry. The "radius" in an overlapping-sphere model, the "effective defect size" on a cubic lattice, and the "cell side" in a cell model are three different definitions; and because m and a₀ multiply together in β = m·tox/a₀, measuring the β-vs-tox slope alone fundamentally cannot separate m from a₀. So whenever you quote an a₀ value, you must quote the geometry and the m it came with, or the number means nothing.
A concrete engineering case: inferring from β = m·N, the conclusion flips with the a₀ assumption

O'Connor et al. measured β = 1.54 (7.8 Å) and 1.82 (8.1 Å) on 8 Å EOT HfO₂ nMOSFETs — far above the 0.8–1 commonly reported at that thickness. Their chain of reasoning:

  1. Keeping SiO₂'s percolation distance, this thickness needs N = 3 traps → β = 1.82 backs out m = 0.61, well above the 0.35–0.4 usually seen.
  2. But high-k has a larger capture cross section, so the percolation distance should be shorter (larger a₀) → N should be 2, or 3 poorly aligned.
  3. If N drops to 2, the inferred m must be higher still. Both routes point to a genuinely anomalous generation rate for this defect, not an unusually long path.
Notice what step 2 did: changing only the trap-size assumption pushed the conclusion about m upward. Same β, different a₀ assumption, and you attribute it to entirely different physics.

⑤ 三個可檢驗的後果

(a) ppm 外推：a₀ 加倍，外推距離也加倍

從 t63 外推到失效率 F，用的是 tF = t63·[−ln(1−F)]1/β。 指數是 1/β，而 β ∝ 1/a₀，所以指數 ∝ a₀ —— a₀ 加倍，外推的對數距離就加倍。

(b) 面積縮放：同一個指數，同一個懲罰

面積縮放律 t63(A₂) = t63(A₁)·(A₁/A₂)1/β 用的也是 1/β。 所以 a₀ 的分歧會同時放大兩次外推的誤差：從 63% 到 ppm 一次，從測試電容到整顆晶片又一次。 兩者相乘，才是最終的保證值。

(c) tox < a₀：滲流圖像自己崩掉

這是 Stathis 1999 最有意思的預測，原文是： “The model predicts a singular behavior when the oxide thickness becomes less than the defect size, because in this limit a single defect near the center of the oxide is sufficient to create a continuous path across the sample.”

N 不可能小於 1
當 tox < a₀ 時，一顆缺陷就能把上下電極連起來，「路徑長度」這個概念消失，β 不再隨厚度下降，而是停在 m。 這意味著 β 對厚度的縮放有一個地板：再薄下去，Weibull 斜率不會無限變小，而是被陷阱生成動力學接管。 Nicollian 等人在 SiON 上量到的現象與此一致 —— EOT 低於 2.2 nm 之後厚度依存性明顯變弱，但到 1.0 nm 仍未消失， 他們的解釋是路徑至少還需要兩顆陷阱（一顆 bulk、一顆界面）。

⑥ high-k 疊層：兩層不同的 a₀ 與 m

實際的 high-k/metal-gate 閘極是疊層：靠矽那一側有一層 SiO₂ 類的界面層（IL），上面才是 HfO₂。 兩層的陷阱種類不同 → 捕獲截面不同 → a₀ 不同；缺陷生成機制不同 → m 也不同。 把兩層放進同一個 β = m·t/a₀，會得到兩個不同的斜率。文獻報告的後果是：崩潰分成兩個階段 （先在 IL 形成滲流路徑，再往 high-k 層傳播），而 Weibull 圖上可能出現從平緩轉到陡峭的兩段斜率， 在大面積元件上尤其明顯。

查證狀態：「IL 主導、兩階段崩潰、雙斜率」這一段是本頁引用的二手整理， 本教材未取得這些論文全文逐字核對（見下方來源表）。可追的原始文獻為 Degraeve et al., IEDM 2005, p. 408（0.9 nm EOT SiO₂/ALD HfO₂ 疊層）與 Kerber et al., IRPS 2009, p. 505（metal gate/high-k 的 TDDB 失效分布）。 本頁逐字核對過的 high-k 內容只有 O'Connor et al., IRPS 2010 那一篇（捕獲截面與滲流距離的論證、β = 1.54 / 1.82）。

檢查你懂了嗎

某製程量到 β = 1.2，tox = 4 nm。這一定代表混進了外質失效嗎？
不一定 —— 這是階段 2 那個問題的修正版。若用 a₀ = 1 nm 且 m = 1，β 應該是 4，1.2 看起來確實異常低，容易歸因到外質失效。但把 m 放回實測的 0.3，β = 0.3 × 4 = 1.2，剛好就是量到的值。也就是說同一個數字可以是「本質 TDDB + 低 m」或「本質 TDDB + 外質尾巴」。要分辨必須看分布形狀（外質失效會在左下角翹出一條尾巴，單一 Weibull 擬不動），而不是只看擬合出來的 β 值。
為什麼只量 β 對 tox 的斜率，沒辦法定出陷阱尺寸 a₀？
因為 β = m·tox/a₀ 裡，m 與 1/a₀ 是乘在一起的。β 對 tox 的斜率只能給你 m/a₀ 這一個組合，不能給你兩個數。要分開必須另外獨立量測 m —— 例如用 SILC（應力誘發漏電流）隨時間的冪次去定 m，再回頭算 a₀。這正是 Nicollian 等人的做法（取 m = 0.26 才得到 bulk 陷阱直徑 ≈ 0.4 nm），也是為什麼不同論文的 a₀ 數值不能互相搬用。
把介電質從 SiO₂ 換成 high-k，理論上 β 會變大還是變小？
單就陷阱尺寸這個因素看，high-k 的位能障較低、缺陷捕獲截面較大 → a₀ 較大 → 一條路徑需要的陷阱數 N 較少 → β 應該變小。但 β = m·N 還有 m 這個因子，而 high-k 的缺陷生成速率可能明顯較高，反而把 β 拉高。O'Connor 等人在 8 Å EOT HfO₂ 上量到 β = 1.54–1.82（高於同厚度常見的 0.8–1），他們的結論就是高 m 蓋過了小 N。所以答案是：兩個因子反向，不能只憑材料猜，必須分別量。
如果持續把 tox 縮下去，β 會一路掉到 0 嗎？
不會。N = tox/a₀ 的地板是 1：當厚度小於陷阱尺寸，一顆缺陷就足以貫穿，「需要幾顆」這個問題消失（Stathis 1999 的奇異行為預測）。此時 β 停在 m，由陷阱生成動力學單獨決定。實務上這代表：超薄介電質的 Weibull 斜率下限不是幾何問題，而是缺陷生成速率問題 —— 改善方向也因此從「加厚」換成「降低生成速率」。

⑤ Three testable consequences

(a) ppm extrapolation: double a₀, double the distance you extrapolate

Extrapolating from t63 to a failure rate F uses tF = t63·[−ln(1−F)]1/β. The exponent is 1/β, and β ∝ 1/a₀, so the exponent ∝ a₀ — double a₀ and you double the logarithmic distance you extrapolate.

(b) Area scaling: same exponent, same penalty

The area-scaling law t63(A₂) = t63(A₁)·(A₁/A₂)1/β uses 1/β as well. So the a₀ disagreement amplifies both extrapolations: once from 63% down to ppm, and again from test capacitor up to full chip. It's the product of the two that sets your final guarantee.

(c) tox < a₀: the percolation picture collapses on itself

This is Stathis's most interesting 1999 prediction, in his own words: “The model predicts a singular behavior when the oxide thickness becomes less than the defect size, because in this limit a single defect near the center of the oxide is sufficient to create a continuous path across the sample.”

N can never go below 1
Once tox < a₀, a single defect bridges both electrodes, the notion of "path length" disappears, and β stops falling with thickness — it parks at m. So β's thickness scaling has a floor: thin the dielectric further and the Weibull slope doesn't shrink without limit, it gets taken over by trap-generation kinetics. Nicollian et al.'s SiON measurements are consistent with this — the thickness dependence weakens markedly below 2.2 nm EOT yet still hasn't vanished at 1.0 nm, which they explain by the path still needing at least two traps (one bulk, one interface).

⑥ High-k stacks: two layers, two different a₀ and m

A real high-k/metal-gate stack is exactly that — a stack: an SiO₂-like interfacial layer (IL) on the silicon side, with HfO₂ above it. The two layers host different trap species → different capture cross sections → different a₀; and different defect-generation mechanisms → different m. Feed both into β = m·t/a₀ and you get two different slopes. The reported consequence is that breakdown proceeds in two phases (percolation paths form in the IL first, then propagate into the high-k), and the Weibull plot can show a transition from a shallow to a steep slope, most visibly on large-area devices.

The traceable primary references are Degraeve et al., IEDM 2005, p. 408 (0.9 nm EOT SiO₂/ALD HfO₂ stacks) and Kerber et al., IRPS 2009, p. 505 (TDDB failure distributions of metal gate/high-k devices). The only high-k content verbatim-verified on this page is O'Connor et al., IRPS 2010 (the capture-cross-section argument and β = 1.54 / 1.82).

Check your understanding

A process measures β = 1.2 at tox = 4 nm. Does that necessarily mean extrinsic failures got mixed in?
Not necessarily — this is the corrected version of that question from Stage 2. With a₀ = 1 nm and m = 1, β should be 4, so 1.2 does look anomalously low and extrinsic failure is the easy explanation. But put m back at a measured 0.3 and β = 0.3 × 4 = 1.2 — exactly the measured value. So the same number can mean "intrinsic TDDB with low m" or "intrinsic TDDB plus an extrinsic tail." Telling them apart requires looking at the distribution shape (extrinsic failures throw an upturned tail at the lower left that a single Weibull can't fit), not just the fitted β.
Why can't measuring the β-vs-tox slope pin down the trap size a₀?
Because in β = m·tox/a₀, m and 1/a₀ are multiplied together. The β-vs-tox slope gives you only the combination m/a₀, which is one number, not two. Separating them requires measuring m independently — for example, fitting the power law of SILC (stress-induced leakage current) over time to get m, then back-calculating a₀. That's exactly what Nicollian et al. did (taking m = 0.26 is what yields a bulk trap diameter ≈ 0.4 nm), and it's why a₀ values from different papers can't be swapped around.
Swap SiO₂ for a high-k — should β go up or down, in theory?
On the trap-size factor alone: high-k has a lower barrier and a larger defect capture cross section → larger a₀ → fewer traps N needed per path → β should go down. But β = m·N also carries m, and high-k defect generation rates can be markedly higher, pushing β back up. O'Connor et al. measured β = 1.54–1.82 on 8 Å EOT HfO₂ (above the 0.8–1 commonly seen at that thickness) and concluded that a high m overwhelmed the small N. So the answer is: the two factors pull in opposite directions — you can't guess from the material, you have to measure them separately.
If you keep scaling tox down, does β fall all the way to 0?
No. N = tox/a₀ has a floor of 1: once the thickness is below the trap size, a single defect suffices to bridge and the question "how many defects" stops meaning anything (Stathis's 1999 singular-behavior prediction). β then parks at m, set by trap-generation kinetics alone. Practically, that means the Weibull-slope floor for ultra-thin dielectrics is not a geometry problem but a defect-generation-rate problem — which also moves the fix from "make it thicker" to "lower the generation rate."
