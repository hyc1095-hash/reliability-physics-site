Stage 2 / 7

Percolation model: watch defects connect into a path

This page isn't an animated illustration — it's a real Monte Carlo simulation running in your browser. We feed in only three assumptions, and the Weibull distribution, β ≈ tox/a₀, and the area-scaling law all emerge on their own — nothing is plugged in from a formula.

整個模型只有三個假設

  1. 把氧化層切成邊長 a₀ ≈ 1 nm 的小格子 —— 這是一個缺陷「佔掉」的體積，跟階段 1 的那些小方格是同一個東西。 所以厚度 tox 的氧化層有 N = tox/a₀ 層。
  2. 電流通過時，每個格子以相同機率、獨立地變成缺陷。缺陷密度隨時間線性增加。
  3. 當缺陷連成一條從上電極貫穿到下電極的通路，崩潰發生。

沒有 Weibull、沒有 β、沒有面積律。這些全部是結果，不是輸入 —— 這正是這個模型有說服力的地方。

The whole model is just three assumptions

  1. Slice the oxide into small cells of side length a₀ ≈ 1 nm — the volume one defect "occupies," the same little squares from Stage 1. So an oxide of thickness tox has N = tox/a₀ layers.
  2. As current flows, each cell turns into a defect independently, with equal probability. Defect density increases linearly with time.
  3. Breakdown happens the instant defects connect into a path from the top electrode all the way to the bottom electrode.

No Weibull, no β, no area law were put in. All of those are outputs, not inputs — that's exactly what makes this model convincing.

實驗一：跑幾百顆虛擬電容，看分布自己長出來

每顆電容都是同樣的材料、同樣的厚度、同樣的應力。唯一的差別是缺陷長在哪裡是隨機的。 按開始，右邊的 Weibull 圖會一點一點被填出來。

實驗三：面積為什麼會吃掉壽命

一顆產品晶片的閘極總面積，可能是測試電容的一萬倍。 面積大 = 骰子擲得多 = 只要任何一處先貫穿就整顆死。這叫最弱環節（weakest link）。

This is exactly why thin oxides are so hard to manage. As tox shrinks from 6 nm to 2 nm, β drops from about 6 to about 2. A small β means a wide spread — the small handful of early failures die far sooner than the median. And what you have to guarantee isn't the median — it's one in a million. The next page works out exactly how heavy that penalty is.

Experiment 3: why area eats into lifetime

順便回答一個常見疑問

問題答案
a₀ 為什麼是 1 nm？ 它是「一個缺陷在電性上等效佔掉多大空間」的尺度，不是原子半徑。 1.0 nm 是本教材為了讓數字好算而選的值，不是文獻共識 —— 實際上不同論文由 β–tox 斜率反推出的 a₀ 從 0.4 nm 到 3 nm 都有，因為每篇用的滲流幾何不同，a₀ 的定義也就不同。 階段 3 專門處理這件事，並解釋為什麼這個分歧能讓 ppm 壽命差十個數量級。
SBD 與 HBD 差在哪？ 第一條路徑形成時，通常只是軟崩潰（SBD） —— 漏電跳一階但電路還能動。 之後焦耳熱把路徑燒寬，才變成硬崩潰（HBD）。薄氧化層的 SBD 到 HBD 之間可以差很久， 所以「什麼叫失效」在薄氧製程是要定義的。
為什麼不用「磨損」來想？ 磨損模型會預測所有元件在差不多的時間壞掉（β 很大，例如 20）。 實測 β 只有 1～6（先進薄氧製程甚至低於 1），代表主宰的是隨機性，不是累積損耗量。

檢查你懂了嗎

模擬裡我們從來沒有輸入「Weibull 分布」，為什麼結果會是 Weibull？
因為 Weibull 分布正是「最弱環節 + 冪次型危險率」的數學後果。氧化層被切成大量獨立的小區塊，只要任一區塊先貫穿就整顆失效 —— 這是取最小值。而單一區塊在時間 t 前貫穿的機率 ≈ p(t)N ∝ tN（要 N 層都變成缺陷）。把「冪次型的個別機率」丟進「取最小值」，得到的就是 Weibull，且形狀參數 β = N。
tox 從 5 nm 縮到 2.5 nm，β 大約會怎麼變？這對規格意味著什麼？
β 會從約 5 掉到約 2.5（減半）。分布變散兩倍以上，1 ppm 壽命與 t63 的比值從 ~6% 掉到 ~0.1% 量級。意味著：同樣的中位數壽命，薄氧化層能提供的 ppm 級保證少了兩個數量級以上。
如果實測到某製程的 β 明顯低於 tox/a₀（例如 tox=4 nm 但 β=1.2），最可能是什麼原因？
有兩種可能，而且都不是「滲流模型錯了」。第一種：陷阱生成冪次 m 小於 1。 本頁的模擬假設缺陷密度隨時間線性增加（等於 m = 1），但實測的 m 常是 0.3 左右， 而完整的關係式是 β = m·tox/a₀ —— 0.3 × 4 = 1.2，剛好就是量到的值（詳見 階段 3）。第二種：混進了外質失效（extrinsic failure） —— 微粒、針孔、電漿損傷、antenna 效應造成的局部薄點。這些缺陷讓一小撮元件遠早於本質壽命就壞掉，在 Weibull 圖上表現為左下角翹起的「早夭尾巴」，整體擬合斜率被拉低。處理方式是雙分布擬合（extrinsic + intrinsic 分開），而不是硬套一條直線。
為什麼滲流模型可以同時解釋 Weibull 分布、β–tox 關係、以及面積縮放律？
因為這三件事都來自同一個假設：缺陷隨機獨立生成 + 只要有一條貫穿路徑就失效。分布形狀來自「取最小值」；β 來自「一條路徑要 N 個缺陷」；面積律來自「面積大＝獨立區塊多」。一個假設推出三個可獨立驗證的結論 —— 這就是好模型的樣子。

The area-scaling law isn't an empirical formula — it's the arithmetic consequence of percolation. Scale the area up by A₂/A₁ and it's the same as rolling A₂/A₁ sets of dice at once; under a Weibull distribution, the distribution of the minimum is still Weibull, just with its characteristic lifetime compressed to t63(A₂) = t63(A₁)·(A₁/A₂)1/β. The smaller β is, the deeper this cut goes — thin oxides lose out again.

A few common questions, answered

QuestionAnswer
Why is a₀ exactly 1 nm? It's the scale of "how much space one defect effectively occupies electrically," not an atomic radius. 1.0 nm is a value this coursework picked to keep the arithmetic clean — it is not a literature consensus. Papers back-calculating a₀ from measured β–tox slopes land anywhere from 0.4 nm to 3 nm, because each uses a different percolation geometry and therefore a different definition of a₀. Stage 3 is devoted to this, and to why that disagreement can move the ppm lifetime by ten orders of magnitude.
What's the difference between SBD and HBD? When the first path forms, it's usually just a soft breakdown (SBD) — leakage jumps a step but the circuit still works. Joule heating later widens the path into a hard breakdown (HBD). The gap between SBD and HBD can be quite long for thin oxides, so "what counts as failure" has to be explicitly defined for thin-oxide processes.
Why not just think of it as "wear-out"? A wear-out model predicts all devices fail around the same time (a large β, say 20). Measured β is only 1–6 — and below 1 for advanced thin-oxide processes — which means randomness dominates, not accumulated wear.

Check your understanding

We never fed "Weibull distribution" into the simulation — why does the result come out Weibull anyway?
Because a Weibull distribution is exactly the mathematical consequence of "weakest link + power-law hazard rate." The oxide is sliced into many independent small blocks, and the instant any one block percolates through, the whole device fails — that's taking a minimum. A single block's probability of percolating before time t is ≈ p(t)N ∝ tN (all N layers need to become defects). Feed that "power-law individual probability" into "take the minimum," and out comes a Weibull distribution with shape parameter β = N.
As tox shrinks from 5 nm to 2.5 nm, roughly how does β change — and what does that mean for specs?
β drops from about 5 to about 2.5 (roughly halved). The distribution widens by more than 2×, and the ratio of 1-ppm lifetime to t63 drops from the ~6% range to the ~0.1% range. In other words: for the same median lifetime, a thin oxide can offer more than two orders of magnitude less ppm-level guarantee.
If a measured process shows β noticeably lower than tox/a₀ (say tox=4 nm but β=1.2), what's the most likely cause?
There are two possibilities, and neither is "the percolation model is wrong." First: the trap-generation exponent m is below 1. This page's simulation assumes defect density grows linearly with time (i.e. m = 1), but measured m is often around 0.3, and the full relation is β = m·tox/a₀ — 0.3 × 4 = 1.2, exactly the measured value (see Stage 3). Second: extrinsic failures have mixed in: particles, pinholes, plasma damage, or antenna-effect local thin spots. These defects make a small subset of devices fail far earlier than the intrinsic lifetime, showing up on a Weibull plot as an upturned "early-death tail" at the lower left, which drags the overall fitted slope down. The fix is a two-population fit (separating extrinsic from intrinsic), not forcing a single straight line through everything.
Why can the percolation model explain the Weibull distribution, the β–tox relationship, and the area-scaling law all at once?
Because all three come from the same assumption: defects form randomly and independently, and a single percolating path is enough to fail the device. Distribution shape comes from "taking a minimum"; β comes from "a path needs N defects"; the area law comes from "more area = more independent blocks." One assumption yields three independently verifiable conclusions — that's what a good model looks like.
