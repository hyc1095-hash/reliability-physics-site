階段 5 / 5 · 判準、外推與防治

對數常態外推，以及跟 TDDB 打架的對策

統計外推：同一套代數，不同的物理理由要記清楚

NBTI 同樣採用對數常態外推，沿用本站 TDDB／HCI 章節的同一套外推式 t(F) = t50 · exp(σ · z(F))。物理理由是乘性退化本質（單顆元件連續參數漂移）， 跟 HCI 是同一類；但 NBTI 特有的是：外推用的時間指數 n 必須來自 應力中（stress-phase，例如 OTF）擬合的值，不能拿恢復後量到的殘餘 ΔVt 去反推 —— 這是階段 3 恢復陷阱在外推環節的直接後果。

防治對策，以及跟 TDDB 的直接衝突

製程層

降低界面態密度、氘退火

提升初始界面品質（更乾淨的介面處理）、氘退火（氘取代氫鈍化 Si 懸鍵，Si-D 鍵斷裂活化能較高， 理論上更耐應力）。

代價：氘退火製程整合複雜度高，且效果在某些疊層上不如平面元件明顯。
氮化（提升 k、抗 TDDB）

介電層氮化是提升 TDDB 抗性的標準做法之一（見 TDDB 章節）， 但氮原子本身會增加界面附近可被電洞捕獲的陷阱位置，使 NBTI 劣化加劇。

降低氮化（抗 NBTI）

降低氮濃度或改變氮分布輪廓能減緩 NBTI，但同時犧牲一部分 TDDB 抗性 —— 兩個機制的製程對策方向直接相反，必須聯合最佳化，不能各自獨立最佳化。

電路層

降低靜態負偏壓佔比、主動輪替偏壓極性

避免電路設計讓特定 pMOS 長時間固定在深負偏壓（例如某些低功耗待機模式下的偏壓策略調整）； 對高風險節點考慮週期性切換偏壓極性，讓元件有機會落入恢復窗口。

代價：電路複雜度增加，且並非所有拓樸都有偏壓極性可切換的空間。

檢查你懂了嗎

為什麼氮化對 TDDB 有利、對 NBTI 不利，是同一個物理量在起作用嗎？
不完全是同一個物理量，但有共同的根源：氮原子取代部分氧原子後，介電層的鍵結網絡改變， 一方面提高了介電層本身抵抗缺陷生成、進而抵抗貫穿路徑形成的能力（對 TDDB 有利）， 另一方面在 Si/介電層界面附近，氮相關的鍵結環境更容易形成可被電洞捕獲的陷阱前驅物 （對 NBTI 不利）。兩者共用「介電層鍵結網絡被氮改變」這個起點，但下游影響的失效機制不同。
如果我的產品 TDDB 判準很寬鬆、NBTI 判準很嚴格，製程選擇上應該怎麼想？
這種情況下可以考慮往降低氮化的方向調整，用犧牲一部分（原本就寬鬆的）TDDB 裕度 去換取 NBTI 的改善。但要注意：TDDB 判準寬鬆可能只是在目前的使用條件與壽命目標下寬鬆， 調整製程後必須重新驗證 TDDB 判準是否仍然滿足，不能只看單一機制的相對裕度就下決定 —— 這正是「聯合最佳化」的實務意涵。

Stage 5 / 5 · Criteria, Extrapolation & Mitigation

Lognormal extrapolation, and a countermeasure that fights TDDB

Statistical extrapolation: the same algebra, but keep the different physical reason straight

NBTI likewise uses lognormal extrapolation, reusing this site's TDDB/HCI chapters' same extrapolation formula t(F) = t50 · exp(σ · z(F)). The physical reason is its multiplicative-degradation nature (continuous parameter drift in a single device), the same category as HCI; but what's unique to NBTI is that the time exponent n used for extrapolation must come from a value fitted during stress (the stress-phase, e.g. OTF) — it can't be back-calculated from the residual ΔVt measured after recovery has already happened — this is the direct consequence of Stage 3's recovery trap showing up in the extrapolation step.

Mitigation, and its direct conflict with TDDB

Process layer

Lowering interface-trap density, deuterium annealing

Improving initial interface quality (cleaner interface processing); deuterium annealing (deuterium replaces hydrogen in passivating Si dangling bonds — the Si-D bond has a higher breaking activation energy, which is theoretically more stress-resistant).

Cost: deuterium annealing adds process-integration complexity, and its effect is less pronounced on some stacks than on planar devices.
Nitridation (raises k, resists TDDB)

Dielectric nitridation is one of the standard ways to improve TDDB resistance (see the TDDB chapter), but the nitrogen atoms themselves add hole-trappable trap sites near the interface, worsening NBTI degradation.

Reducing nitridation (resists NBTI)

Lowering the nitrogen concentration or changing its distribution profile eases NBTI, but sacrifices some TDDB resistance in the process — the two mechanisms' process countermeasures point in directly opposite directions, so they must be jointly optimized, not optimized independently.

Circuit layer

Reducing time spent under static negative bias, actively alternating bias polarity

Avoiding circuit designs that leave a specific pMOS fixed at deep negative bias for long stretches (e.g. adjusting bias strategy under certain low-power standby modes); for high-risk nodes, consider periodically flipping bias polarity so the device gets a chance to fall into a recovery window.

Cost: added circuit complexity, and not every topology has room to flip bias polarity.

Check your understanding

Nitridation helps TDDB and hurts NBTI — is it the same physical quantity acting in both cases?
Not exactly the same physical quantity, but they share a common root: once nitrogen atoms replace some oxygen atoms, the dielectric's bonding network changes. On one hand, this raises the dielectric's own ability to resist defect generation and, in turn, resist the formation of a percolation path (good for TDDB); on the other hand, near the Si/dielectric interface, the nitrogen-related bonding environment more readily forms trap precursors that holes can capture (bad for NBTI). Both effects share the same starting point — "the dielectric's bonding network is altered by nitrogen" — but the downstream failure mechanisms they affect are different.
If my product's TDDB criterion has plenty of margin but the NBTI criterion is tight, how should that shape the process choice?
In that case it's worth considering a shift toward less nitridation, trading away some of the (already ample) TDDB margin to buy an NBTI improvement. But be careful: a loose TDDB criterion may only be loose under the current use condition and lifetime target — after changing the process, TDDB must be re-verified to confirm it's still met. You can't make the call just by looking at one mechanism's relative margin in isolation — that's exactly what "joint optimization" means in practice.
