一分鐘版

汲極電壓夠高時，通道靠近汲極的一段會「夾止」（pinch-off）。 所有多出來的電壓幾乎全部落在這一小段上 —— 強電場就集中在那裡，損傷也就只發生在那裡。 這是 HCI 最重要的一個特徵：它是局部的。

Five steps: watching the damage happen

The last page told "one electron's story." This page puts it back into the real device structure, walking through step by step: where the field gets strong, where trouble starts, where the defects end up.

The 1-minute version

Once the drain voltage is high enough, the stretch of channel near the drain "pinches off." Almost all of the extra voltage drops across this one short stretch — that's where the strong field concentrates, and that's the only place damage happens. This is HCI's single most important trait: it's local.

四種熱載子注入模式

同樣是「熱載子」，依照 VG 與 VD 的組合，主導的機制不一樣。 初學階段先記住前兩種就夠了 —— 它們涵蓋絕大多數實際應力條件。

本教材的模型涵蓋 DAHC 與 CHE；SHE 與 off-state / GIDL 未納入 —— 所以把 VG 調到 0 時，模型只會顯示「幾乎沒有電流」， 而真實元件在那裡仍有 GIDL 造成的退化。知道模型的邊界，跟知道模型本身一樣重要。

最該帶走的一句話

HCI 的損傷集中在汲極端那幾十奈米。 這個「局部性」會直接影響後面的量測：線性區量測（Idlin）時損傷區正好卡在電流路徑上，非常敏感； 飽和區量測（Idsat）時電流由源極端決定，就不敏感。同一顆元件，兩個指標的退化量可以差 3 到 10 倍。

檢查你懂了嗎

為什麼步驟 1（線性區）幾乎不會有 HCI？
因為 VD < Vdsat，通道從源極到汲極都完整反轉，電壓平均分攤在整條通道上，沒有任何一段的電場特別強。沒有高場區就沒有熱載子。

這解釋了一件實務上很有用的事：類比開關在導通期間幾乎不產生 HCI，損傷幾乎全發生在開關轉態的那一瞬間 —— 那時 VD 還沒塌下來、通道卻已經開始導通。
為什麼電洞會往基板跑，形成 Isub？
撞擊游離產生一對電子與電洞。電子被汲極的正電壓吸走（併入 ID），電洞則被推向電位最低的地方 —— 也就是接地的基板。

這件事在工程上非常好用：Isub 是唯一能從外部直接量到的『高場強度指標』。所以 HCI 的標準做法是掃 Isub–VG 找峰值，把它當作應力條件。
界面態 Nit 和氧化層裡的陷阱電荷有什麼不同？
界面態在 Si/SiO₂ 介面上，能量位置在矽的能隙裡，可以隨閘極電壓充放電 —— 它會同時造成 Vt 漂移和遷移率下降（因為它散射通道裡的載子）。

氧化層陷阱電荷在氧化層內部，是被捕獲的固定電荷 —— 主要造成 Vt 平移。

兩者都會發生，但 NMOS 的 HCI 退化通常由界面態主導，這也是為什麼轉導 gm 的衰退是很靈敏的指標。
為什麼說「損傷是局部的」這件事會影響量測？
因為損傷只在汲極端那一小塊。線性區量測（VD 很小）時整條通道均勻反轉，損傷區直接串在電流路徑上，等效於一段額外電阻 —— 極度敏感。飽和區量測（VG=VD=高壓）時電流由源極端的位障決定，汲極端本來就在夾止區、載子少 —— 相對不敏感。

進階技巧：把 source 和 drain 對調再量一次（reverse mode），讓損傷端變成 source，敏感度會再放大 —— 這是定位損傷位置的標準手法。

Four hot-carrier injection modes

They're all "hot carriers," but which mechanism dominates depends on the VG/VD combination. At the beginner stage, the first two are enough to remember — they cover the vast majority of real stress conditions.

ModeBias conditionWhat happensWhen it matters most
DAHC
Drain avalanche hot carriers		 VG ≈ VD/2 The product of channel current and peak field is maximized; impact ionization is most intense, generating the most electron-hole pairs and the fastest interface-trap growth. Isub peaks here. Worst case for thick-oxide / long-channel NMOS; JESD28's standard stress condition
CHE
Channel hot electrons		 VG ≈ VD The oxide field pulls hot electrons toward the gate most efficiently. IG peaks here. Thin-oxide / short-channel devices; monitored via IG in practice
SHE
Substrate hot electrons		 Applied substrate bias Carriers are accelerated by the depletion-region field from the substrate before injection. Mainly used for characterization, not a natural operating condition. Lab-bench mechanism isolation
Off-state
Off-state		 VG ≈ 0, high VD The channel is off, but the gate-drain overlap region has a very strong vertical field, causing band-to-band tunneling (GIDL). A different mechanism from the first three. High-voltage / I/O devices, circuits idle for long periods

This course's model covers DAHC and CHE; SHE and off-state / GIDL are not included — so when you turn VG down to 0, the model will simply show "almost no current," while a real device would still be degrading there from GIDL. Knowing a model's boundaries matters as much as knowing the model itself.

The one line to remember

HCI damage is concentrated in the tens of nanometers near the drain. That "locality" directly shapes how you measure it afterward: a linear-region measurement (Idlin) puts the damaged region right in the current path, so it's very sensitive; a saturation-region measurement (Idsat) is dominated by the source-side barrier, so it's much less sensitive. On the same device, the two metrics' degradation can differ by 3-10×.

Check your understanding

Why does Step 1 (linear region) show almost no HCI?
Because VD < Vdsat, the channel stays fully inverted from source to drain, and the voltage is spread evenly along its whole length — no single stretch has an especially strong field. No high-field region means no hot carriers.

This explains something practically useful: an analog switch generates almost no HCI while it's on — nearly all the damage happens in the instant of switching, when VD hasn't collapsed yet but the channel has already started conducting.
Why do holes flow to the substrate, forming Isub?
Impact ionization produces an electron-hole pair. The electron gets pulled away by the drain's positive voltage (joining ID), while the hole gets pushed toward the lowest potential available — the grounded substrate.

This turns out to be extremely useful in practice: Isub is the only externally measurable proxy for "how strong the local field is." That's why the standard HCI procedure is to sweep Isub-VG, find the peak, and use that as the stress condition.
What's the difference between interface traps Nit and oxide trapped charge?
Interface traps sit right at the Si/SiO₂ interface, with energy levels inside silicon's bandgap, and can charge and discharge as the gate voltage changes — they cause both a Vt shift and mobility degradation (since they scatter carriers in the channel).

Oxide trapped charge sits inside the bulk of the oxide as captured fixed charge — it mainly causes a Vt shift.

Both occur, but NMOS HCI degradation is usually dominated by interface traps, which is why the drop in transconductance gm is such a sensitive indicator.
Why does "the damage is local" matter for measurement?
Because the damage only exists in that one small region near the drain. A linear-region measurement (small VD) inverts the whole channel uniformly, putting the damaged region directly in series with the current path — like an extra resistor — extremely sensitive. A saturation-region measurement (VG=VD=high) is dominated by the source-side barrier; the drain end is already pinched off with few carriers there anyway — comparatively insensitive.

Advanced trick: swap source and drain and re-measure (reverse mode), turning the damaged end into the source — this amplifies the sensitivity further, and is the standard technique for locating where the damage sits.
