電遷移（Electromigration）
可靠度物理，從零開始

EM 發生在後段金屬導線裡，不在電晶體本身——這是它跟 HCI、TDDB、NBTI 最大的不同：故事的舞台從矽換成了金屬線。 更關鍵的是驅動力也不一樣：不是電場，而是電流本身帶來的動量——你可以把它想成一陣持續吹過金屬原子的「風」。 這份教材分四個階段：直覺、Black 方程式與材料活化能、Blech 長度的「免疫」效應、以及統計外推與防治。 每一頁都短，一次只講一件事，卡住了隨時可以往回翻。

一句話版本

導電電子與金屬原子不斷碰撞，把動量傳給原子——這就是「電子風」。電流密度夠高時， 這股風會讓原子沿電流方向淨位移，一顆一顆被搬走：陰極端因此逐漸掏空成孔洞（最終斷路）， 陽極端則可能因為原子越堆越多而擠出突起（可能短路到旁邊的線）。

Electromigration (EM)
reliability physics, from zero

EM happens in the back-end metal wiring, not in the transistor itself — the biggest difference from HCI, TDDB, and NBTI: the stage shifts from silicon to metal lines. Even more important, the driving force is different too: not an electric field, but momentum carried by the current itself — think of it as a steady "wind" blowing through the metal atoms. This course breaks it into four stages: intuition, the Black equation and material activation energy, the Blech-length "immunity" effect, and statistical extrapolation with mitigation. Every page is short and covers exactly one idea — you can always flip back.

The 30-second version

Conducting electrons keep colliding with metal atoms, handing over momentum — that's the "electron wind." Once the current density is high enough, this wind produces a net drift of atoms along the current direction, carrying them away one by one: the cathode end gradually hollows out into a void (eventually an open circuit), while the anode end can pile atoms up into a hillock (potentially shorting to a neighboring line).
