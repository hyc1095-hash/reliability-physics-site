L0 · 熱身

電流到底是什麼

所有半導體電流公式都是同一件事的變形：電流 = 有多少電荷 × 它們跑多快。

I = q · n · v · A
I電流A（安培）
q基本電荷1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
n載子密度每立方公分有幾顆電子
v漂移速度電子平均往前跑多快
A截面積電流通過的面積

白話：水管裡的水流量 = 水的濃度 × 流速 × 管子粗細。電流一模一樣。

v = µ · E
µ遷移率cm²/V·s，「同樣的電場能推多快」
E電場V/cm

白話：電場推電子，但電子一直被晶格撞（就是階段 1 講的散射）， 所以不會一直加速，而是達到一個穩定的平均速度。µ 就是這個「推力換速度」的比例常數。
兩式合起來： I = q·n·µ·E·A —— 這就是歐姆定律的微觀版本。 注意最後一項：電流正比於電場。這件事到了高場區會壞掉 —— 見 L1 最後一格。

L1 · 基礎

MOSFET 的電流

MOSFET 只是把上面的東西套進一個特殊幾何：載子被閘極吸到表面形成一層薄薄的通道。 所以要問兩件事：閘極吸來多少電荷？電場推它們跑多快？

Qinv = Cox · ( VG − Vt )   ,   Cox = εox / tox
Qinv反轉層電荷密度C/cm²，單位面積吸來多少電荷
Cox閘極氧化層電容F/cm²
Vt臨界電壓開始形成通道所需的閘壓
VG − Vt過驅動電壓 Vgt超過門檻多少，決定吸來多少電荷

白話：閘極 + 氧化層 + 通道就是一個平行板電容。給它超過 Vt 的電壓， 它就在下面存一層電荷。氧化層越薄（tox 小）→ 電容越大 → 同樣電壓吸來越多電荷 → 電流越大。 這是縮小 tox 的動機 —— 但階段 3 說過，它同時讓 HCI 惡化。

ID = (W/L) · µ Cox [ ( VG − Vt ) VD − VD²/2 ]

這是線性區（三極管區）的電流。怎麼來的？沿通道每一點的電荷是 Q(y) = Cox(VG − Vt − V(y))，因為通道電位 V(y) 從源極的 0 升到汲極的 VD， 越靠近汲極，閘極對通道的「有效驅動」越弱、電荷越少。沿通道積分就得到這條式子。
VD 很小時 VD²/2 可以忽略 → ID ≈ (W/L)µCoxVgt·VD， 電流正比於 VD，元件就像一個電阻。這就是 Ron 的由來。

VD = VG − Vt   ⟹   汲極端電荷歸零  ⟹  夾止（pinch-off）

這是整個 HCI 故事的起點。把 VD 一直加大，汲極端的有效驅動 VG − Vt − V(y) 會先歸零。那一點之後通道就沒有反轉層了 —— 這就是階段 2 步驟 2 看到的夾止。
再加更多電壓，多出來的部分全部落在夾止區這一小段上。強電場、熱載子、HCI，全都從這裡開始。
長通道時 Vdsat = VG − Vt。飽和電流則是把上式代回去： ID,sat = (W/2L)·µCox·(VG−Vt)²。

Vdsat = Vgt · EsatL Vgt + EsatL   ,   Esat = 2vsat / µ
vsat飽和速度≈ 1 × 10⁷ cm/s，電子跑不過的上限
Esat速度飽和的臨界電場超過它，v = µE 就不成立了

為什麼需要這條修正？因為 L0 的 v = µE 假設速度可以無限上升，但實際上電子最快只能跑到 vsat。 短通道元件很容易就超過 Esat，這時 Vdsat 會比 VG−Vt 小很多， 夾止提早發生，夾止區的壓降 VD−Vdsat 就更大 —— HCI 更嚴重。
兩個極限值得記：L 很長時 EsatL ≫ Vgt，Vdsat → Vgt（回到長通道結果）； L 很短時 Vdsat → EsatL，與 VG 幾乎無關。

L0 · Warm-up

What current actually is

Every semiconductor current equation is a variation on the same idea: current = how much charge × how fast it moves.

I = q · n · v · A
ICurrentA (amperes)
qElementary charge1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
nCarrier densityelectrons per cubic centimeter
vDrift velocityhow fast electrons move on average
ACross-sectional areathe area current flows through

Plain language: flow rate in a pipe = water concentration × flow speed × pipe cross-section. Current is exactly the same idea.

v = µ · E
µMobilitycm²/V·s — "how fast the same field can push"
EElectric fieldV/cm

Plain language: the field pushes electrons, but they keep getting knocked around by the lattice (the scattering from Stage 1), so they don't keep accelerating forever — they settle into a steady average speed. µ is the proportionality constant that converts "push" into "speed."
Combining both equations: I = q·n·µ·E·A — the microscopic version of Ohm's law. Notice the last term: current is proportional to the field. That breaks down at high field — see the last box in L1.

L1 · Basics

The MOSFET's current

A MOSFET just drops the ideas above into a specific geometry: carriers get pulled to the surface by the gate, forming a thin channel. So there are two questions to ask: how much charge does the gate pull in? How fast does the field push it along?

Qinv = Cox · ( VG − Vt )   ,   Cox = εox / tox
QinvInversion-layer charge densityC/cm², how much charge is pulled in per unit area
CoxGate oxide capacitanceF/cm²
VtThreshold voltagethe gate voltage needed to start forming a channel
VG − VtOverdrive voltage Vgthow far past threshold — sets how much charge gets pulled in

Plain language: gate + oxide + channel is just a parallel-plate capacitor. Apply a voltage above Vt and it stores a layer of charge underneath. Thinner oxide (smaller tox) → higher capacitance → more charge pulled in at the same voltage → more current. That's the motivation for shrinking tox — but as Stage 3 showed, it also makes HCI worse.

ID = (W/L) · µ Cox [ ( VG − Vt ) VD − VD²/2 ]

This is the current in the linear (triode) region. Where does it come from? The charge at each point along the channel is Q(y) = Cox(VG − Vt − V(y)); since the channel potential V(y) rises from 0 at the source to VD at the drain, the closer to the drain, the weaker the gate's "effective drive" on the channel, and the less charge there is. Integrating along the channel gives this equation.
When VD is small, VD²/2 is negligible → ID ≈ (W/L)µCoxVgt·VD, current proportional to VD — the device behaves like a resistor. That's where Ron comes from.

VD = VG − Vt   ⟹   charge at the drain hits zero  ⟹  pinch-off

This is where the whole HCI story begins. Keep raising VD, and the effective drive at the drain, VG − Vt − V(y), hits zero first. Past that point, there's no inversion layer left — the pinch-off you saw in Stage 2, Step 2.
Add still more voltage, and all of the extra drops across this one short pinch-off stretch. Strong field, hot carriers, HCI — it all starts right here.
For a long channel, Vdsat = VG − Vt. The saturation current comes from plugging that back in: ID,sat = (W/2L)·µCox·(VG−Vt)².

Vdsat = Vgt · EsatL Vgt + EsatL   ,   Esat = 2vsat / µ
vsatSaturation velocity≈ 1 × 10⁷ cm/s, the hard ceiling on electron speed
EsatVelocity-saturation critical fieldpast this, v = µE no longer holds

Why is this correction needed? Because L0's v = µE assumes speed can rise without limit, but electrons actually top out at vsat. Short-channel devices easily exceed Esat, and when they do, Vdsat ends up much smaller than VG−Vt, pinch-off happens earlier, and the pinch-off voltage drop VD−Vdsat ends up larger — HCI gets worse.
Two limits worth remembering: for a long L, EsatL ≫ Vgt, so Vdsat → Vgt (back to the long-channel result); for a short L, Vdsat → EsatL, almost independent of VG.

動手看：Isub–VD 與 VdsatTry it: ID–VD and Vdsat

虛線是 Vdsat 的軌跡 —— 它左邊是線性區（式 1.3），右邊是飽和區。 把 L 調短，看虛線怎麼往左移、飽和來得多早。The dashed line traces Vdsat — to its left is the linear region (Eq. 1.3), to its right is saturation. Shorten L and watch the dashed line move left, saturating earlier.

L2 · 進階

汲極端的電場（階段 3 的複習）

知道夾止區的壓降是 VD − Vdsat 之後，還缺一件事：它被擠在多短的距離裡？

l = √( 3 · tox · xj )

特徵長度。電力線往閘極與基板漏出去的尺度。3 來自 εSi/εox ≈ 3。 式子裡沒有 L —— 這是階段 3 的重點。

Em ≈ VD − Vdsat l

峰值電場 = 壓降 ÷ 特徵長度。就這麼簡單。 （完整式是 Em = √(Esat² + ((VD−Vdsat)/l)²)，但通常第二項遠大於第一項。）
注意這裡怎麼把 L1 接上來：Vdsat 來自 L1 的元件公式，l 來自幾何。兩個一除就是電場。

L3 · 目標

損傷模型

有了 Em，剩下的全部是階段 1 那個「qλE 對上能量門檻」的量化版。

λ(T) = λ0 · tanh( Ep / 2kT )

整個模型裡溫度唯一的入口。λ0 ≈ 10.4 nm，Ep ≈ 0.063 eV（矽的光學聲子能量）。 高溫 → 聲子多 → 電子容易被撞 → λ 短。

B = φ / (q·λ)

把「能量門檻」翻譯成「電場門檻」。階段 1 的三道門檻各自對應一個 B：
Bi（游離，φi=1.3 eV）· Bg（進閘極，φb=3.1 eV）· Bit（打斷 Si–H，φit=3.7 eV）。
B 越大代表越難發生。因為 B ∝ 1/λ，溫度升高會讓三個 B 一起變大 —— 這就是負活化能的來源。

αi(E) = Ai · e−Bi/E   ⟹   Isub/ID = ∫0ΔL αi(E(y)) dy

αi = 電子每走 1 cm 平均造成幾次游離。那個 e−B/E 就是階段 1 賽道動畫裡的「運氣」。 沿高場區積分，就得到可以從外部量到的 Isub/ID。
因為 αi 隨 E 指數上升、而 E(y) 又是 cosh，這個積分幾乎全由最後幾十奈米貢獻 —— 損傷局部化的數學來源。

dNit/dt ∝ ( ID/W ) · e−Bit/Em

真正在乎的東西。「有多少電子在嘗試」（ID/W）×「每個成功打斷 Si–H 的機率」（指數項）。 前者線性、後者指數 —— 所以後者主導一切。這也是階段 1 那句「HCI 是電場問題不是電流問題」的數學表達。

把兩條式子相除，m 就掉出來了

ln( Isub/ID ) = −Bi/Em + 常數
ln( dNit/dt ÷ (ID/W) ) = −Bit/Em + 常數
兩式相除，Em 消掉 ⟹   dNit/dt ∝ (ID/W) · ( Isub/ID )Bit/Bi
這就是教科書上的 Age function

而那個「大約等於 3 的經驗指數」m，其實是 m = Bit/Bi = (φit/qλ) ÷ (φi/qλ) = φit/φi = —。λ 完全消掉了。

m 是兩個能量門檻的比值，不是配出來的參數。 這也解釋了為什麼不同文獻的 m 落在 2.5–4 之間 —— 因為大家用的 φ 值不一樣。

最後一步：活化能為什麼是負的

dNit/dt ∝ (ID/W) · exp[ − φit / ( q · λ(T) · Em ) ]

把 λ(T) 代進去，溫度的角色就一目了然：T ↑ → λ ↓ → 分母變小 → 指數的負值變大 → 損傷率下降。 寫成 Arrhenius 形式 rate ∝ e−Ea/kT，得到的 Ea 就是負的。

L2 · Intermediate

The field at the drain (a recap of Stage 3)

Now that you know the pinch-off region's voltage drop is VD − Vdsat, one thing is still missing: how short a distance is it squeezed into?

l = √( 3 · tox · xj )

Characteristic length. The length scale over which field lines leak into the gate and substrate. The 3 comes from εSi/εox ≈ 3. There's no L in the equation — the whole point of Stage 3.

Em ≈ VD − Vdsat l

Peak field = voltage drop ÷ characteristic length. That simple. (The full form is Em = √(Esat² + ((VD−Vdsat)/l)²), but the second term usually dominates.)
Notice how this connects back to L1: Vdsat comes from L1's device equations, l comes from geometry. Divide one by the other and you get the field.

L3 · Destination

The damage model

With Em in hand, everything left is just the quantitative version of Stage 1's "qλE versus an energy threshold."

λ(T) = λ0 · tanh( Ep / 2kT )

The only place temperature enters the whole model. λ0 ≈ 10.4 nm, Ep ≈ 0.063 eV (silicon's optical phonon energy). Higher temperature → more phonons → electrons scatter more easily → shorter λ.

B = φ / (q·λ)

Translating an "energy threshold" into a "field threshold." Stage 1's three thresholds each get their own B:
Bi (ionization, φi=1.3 eV) · Bg (into the gate, φb=3.1 eV) · Bit (breaks Si–H, φit=3.7 eV).
A larger B means it's harder to happen. Since B ∝ 1/λ, raising temperature makes all three B's grow together — the origin of the negative activation energy.

αi(E) = Ai · e−Bi/E   ⟹   Isub/ID = ∫0ΔL αi(E(y)) dy

αi = the average number of ionization events an electron causes per cm traveled. That e−B/E is exactly the "luck" from Stage 1's track animation. Integrating along the high-field region gives the externally measurable Isub/ID.
Because αi rises exponentially with E, and E(y) is itself a cosh, this integral is dominated almost entirely by the last few tens of nanometers — the mathematical source of the damage's locality.

dNit/dt ∝ ( ID/W ) · e−Bit/Em

The thing you actually care about. "How many electrons are attempting it" (ID/W) × "the probability each one successfully breaks a Si–H bond" (the exponential term). The former is linear, the latter exponential — so the exponential dominates everything. This is the mathematical expression of Stage 1's "HCI is a field problem, not a current problem."

Divide the two equations, and m falls right out

ln( Isub/ID ) = −Bi/Em + constant
ln( dNit/dt ÷ (ID/W) ) = −Bit/Em + constant
Divide the two equations, Em cancels out ⟹   dNit/dt ∝ (ID/W) · ( Isub/ID )Bit/Bi
This is the textbook Age function

And that "empirical exponent roughly equal to 3," m, turns out to be m = Bit/Bi = (φit/qλ) ÷ (φi/qλ) = φit/φi = —. λ cancels out completely.

m is the ratio of two energy thresholds, not a fitted parameter. That also explains why different papers report m somewhere between 2.5 and 4 — everyone uses slightly different φ values.

Last step: why the activation energy is negative

dNit/dt ∝ (ID/W) · exp[ − φit / ( q · λ(T) · Em ) ]

Plug in λ(T), and temperature's role becomes obvious: T ↑ → λ ↓ → the denominator shrinks → the negative exponent grows larger → the damage rate falls. Written in Arrhenius form, rate ∝ e−Ea/kT, the resulting Ea comes out negative.

檢查你懂了嗎

式 1.3 裡那個 −VD²/2 是哪來的？
來自「通道電位沿路上升」。沿通道每一點的反轉層電荷是 Q(y) = Cox(VG − Vt − V(y))，越靠近汲極、V(y) 越高、電荷越少。把 Q(y) 沿通道積分，第一項給出 VgtVD，電荷遞減的修正就給出 −VD²/2。

把 VD 一直加大到 Vgt，這兩項剛好抵消一半 —— 汲極端的電荷正好歸零，那就是夾止。
為什麼短通道元件的 Vdsat 幾乎與 VG 無關？
看式 1.6：Vdsat = Vgt·EsatL/(Vgt + EsatL)。當 L 很短，EsatL ≪ Vgt，分母被 Vgt 主導，整個式子 → EsatL。

物理上：短通道時電子早在通道中段就已經跑到速度上限，再加閘壓也快不起來。後果：夾止區的壓降 VD−Vdsat 幾乎等於 VD，HCI 特別嚴重。
L0 說 v = µE，但 L1 最後又說這條會壞掉。到底哪個對？
兩個都對，只是適用範圍不同。E ≪ Esat 時 v = µE 成立（低場）；E 接近或超過 Esat 時速度飽和在 vsat，v = µE 會高估。

這是很好的一課：每條公式都有適用範圍。式 1.6 的存在就是為了把這件事縫進 MOSFET 模型裡。同樣地，L2 的 pseudo-2D 假設「高場區 ≪ 通道長度」，ΔL/L 太大時也會失準。
為什麼 m 的推導裡 λ 會消掉？這代表什麼？
因為 Bi 和 Bit 都正比於 1/λ，相除時 λ 就約掉了。

意義很重要：m 與溫度無關。溫度會改變 Isub 也會改變損傷率，但「兩者之間的冪次關係」不會變。所以你可以在一個溫度萃取 m，用在其他溫度 —— 這正是 Age function 在工程上好用的原因。
如果我只記得一條式子，該記哪條？
Em ≈ (VD − Vdsat) / l。

它同時把三件事接在一起：左邊 Vdsat 來自 L1 的元件物理（跟 VG、L、µ 有關）、分母 l 來自製程幾何（tox、xj）、而它的輸出 Em 是 L3 每一條指數式的分母。把這條記住，你就能對任何參數變化做出正確的定性判斷。

Check your understanding

Where does the −VD²/2 in Eq. 1.3 come from?
From "the channel potential rises along its length." The inversion-layer charge at each point along the channel is Q(y) = Cox(VG − Vt − V(y)); the closer to the drain, the higher V(y) is, and the less charge there is. Integrating Q(y) along the channel, the first term gives VgtVD, and the correction for that decreasing charge gives −VD²/2.

Keep raising VD all the way to Vgt, and these two terms exactly cancel by half — the charge at the drain hits exactly zero, and that's pinch-off.
Why is a short-channel device's Vdsat nearly independent of VG?
Look at Eq. 1.6: Vdsat = Vgt·EsatL/(Vgt + EsatL). When L is short, EsatL ≪ Vgt, the denominator is dominated by Vgt, and the whole expression → EsatL.

Physically: in a short channel, electrons already hit their speed limit partway through the channel, so more gate voltage can't push them any faster. Consequence: the pinch-off voltage drop VD−Vdsat ends up nearly equal to VD, and HCI gets especially severe.
L0 says v = µE, but L1 then says this breaks down. Which one is right?
Both are, they just apply in different ranges. When E ≪ Esat, v = µE holds (low field); when E is near or above Esat, velocity saturates at vsat, and v = µE overestimates it.

A good lesson here: every equation has a range where it applies. Eq. 1.6 exists specifically to stitch that fact into the MOSFET model. Likewise, L2's pseudo-2D model assumes "the high-field region ≪ the channel length," and it also breaks down when ΔL/L gets too large.
Why does λ cancel out in the derivation of m — and what does that mean?
Because both Bi and Bit are proportional to 1/λ, so λ cancels when you divide one by the other.

The significance matters: m is independent of temperature. Temperature changes both Isub and the damage rate, but the "power-law relationship between the two" stays fixed. That means you can extract m at one temperature and use it at another — exactly why the Age function is so useful in practice.
If I only remember one equation, which should it be?
Em ≈ (VD − Vdsat) / l.

It ties three things together at once: Vdsat on the left comes from L1's device physics (depends on VG, L, µ), the denominator l comes from process geometry (tox, xj), and its output Em is the denominator in every one of L3's exponentials. Remember this one equation, and you can correctly reason qualitatively about the effect of any parameter change.
