階段 5 / 7 · HCI 規格

HCI 的可靠度規格

前面四頁講物理。這一頁只講一件事：產業如何把 HCI 的物理變成可以簽名的規格 —— 判定準則怎麼定、應力條件怎麼選、溫度等級代表什麼， 以及那個把整份教材串起來的陷阱：產品級的高溫測試，抓不到 HCI。

一分鐘版
  • HCI 有專屬的量測標準：JESD28 / JESD28-1，做的是晶圓層級的 DC 應力，不是封裝後測試。
  • 監測四個參數：VTH、GM、IDLIN、IDSAT。 壽命定義為參數變化 10%，VTH 則是變化 50 mV。
  • 應力條件的傳統做法：掃 Isub–VG 找峰值，在那裡施加 DC 應力，多電壓外推。
  • AEC-Q100 的溫度等級界定操作範圍，但 HCI 的 worst corner 一律在低溫端（多數車規是 −40 °C）。
  • 產品級 HTOL 在 125 °C 跑 1000 小時 —— 對負活化能的 HCI 而言那是減速，不是加速。

HCI 在標準體系裡的位置

可靠度標準有很多份，但跟 HCI 直接有關的只有下面這條線。其他（封裝完整性、ESD、溫度循環等）與 HCI 無關，這裡不列。

一句話

HCI 是「製程級」的問題，不是「產品級」的問題。 它在晶圓上用專用結構量、用模型外推、交給設計端使用。 等到封裝後的 HTOL 才想抓它，時機太晚、機制上也抓不到。

判定準則：什麼叫「壞了」

HCI 不會讓元件突然不動，它是連續的參數漂移。所以「失效」必須人為定義一條線。

標準的監測參數與壽命定義

監測四個參數：臨界電壓 VTH、轉導 GM、線性區汲極電流 IDLIN、飽和區汲極電流 IDSAT。
壽命定義為參數變化 10%；若是 VTH，則定義為相對未應力值變化 50 mV。

為什麼是這四個參數

參數對 HCI 的敏感度物理原因 / 對應的電路功能
IDLIN最敏感 低 VD 量測時整條通道均勻反轉，汲極端的損傷區直接串在電流路徑上， 等效於一段額外電阻加上局部 Vt shift。
對應功能：Ron —— 類比開關、取樣開關、LDO pass device、交換電容電路。
GM敏感 界面態會散射通道載子、降低有效遷移率。
對應功能：放大器增益、GBW、slew rate。
VTH中等 陷阱電荷造成的平移。用絕對值（50 mV）而非百分比，因為 Vt 本身的絕對值才是電路在乎的。
對應功能：偏壓點、matched pair 的 offset。
IDSAT不敏感 VG=VD=高壓時電流由源極端的位障與速度飽和決定； 汲極端本來就在夾止區、載子濃度低，損傷區的貢獻小。
對應功能：驅動能力、延遲、電流源。
最常被誤解的一點

ΔIDLIN ≫ ΔIDSAT（文獻常見比值 3–10 倍）是 HCI 局部損傷的必然結果，不是元件異常。

所以「IDLIN 先撞線、IDSAT 還很寬鬆」是正常現象。 真正該問的不是「為什麼 IDLIN 這麼差」，而是「這顆元件在應用裡工作在哪個區？」—— 線性區操作（開關、pass device）就該卡 IDLIN；飽和區操作（放大器、電流源）的功能參數是 IDSAT 與 GM， IDLIN 不是它的功能限制。

進階技巧：把 source 與 drain 對調再量一次（reverse mode），讓損傷端變成源極，敏感度會再放大 —— 這是定位損傷位置、確認「損傷確實在汲極端」的標準手法。

應力條件怎麼定

HCI 應力測試的流程，本質上就是階段 4 那條 Age function 的實驗版：

步驟做什麼為什麼
1在目標 VD 下掃 VG，量 Isub–VG 曲線，找出峰值位置 Isub 是唯一能從外部即時量到的高場強度指標
2在 peak Isub 的 VG 下施加 DC 應力，週期性中斷量四個參數（MSM：measure–stress–measure） DC 條件可重複、可比較；HCI 無回復，中斷量測不會失真
3用多個 VD 重複，得到多組壽命 τ 建立電壓加速關係，才有外推的依據
4把 ln(τ·ID/W) 對 ln(Isub/ID) 作圖，斜率就是 −m m 應從自己的資料萃取，不要直接套 3
5用 Age function 外推到使用條件的 VD 與溫度 這一步的不確定性最大 —— 見下方但書

三個容易被問倒的設計選擇

問題答案
為什麼用 DC 應力，
而不是模擬真實的 AC 波形？		 因為 HCI 是準靜態的：載子加熱在次皮秒尺度完成，而且沒有回復效應。 所以任意 AC 波形造成的損傷，等於沿波形對 DC 損傷率作積分。
DC 應力提供的是模型參數；AC 的部分交給設計端的 reliability simulator 沿自己的波形積分。 用 DC 建模、用模型算 AC —— 這樣一組資料可以服務所有客戶電路。
為什麼在晶圓層級做，
不等封裝後的 HTOL？		 三個理由。機制上抓不到（HTOL 在 125 °C，對負活化能的 HCI 是減速）； 時機太晚（HTOL 是產品級測試，發現時製程已定版、客戶已投片）； 交付物不對（客戶要的是能放進 reliability simulator 的模型，不是一句「我們過了」）。
為什麼在 peak Isub？
那裡是損傷最大的地方嗎？		 不完全是。peak Isub 是訊號最好量、加速最穩定的條件，是傳統做法。 但損傷率 ∝ Isubm/IDm−1，它的峰值會落在比 peak Isub 更低的 VG。
兩者不同是正常的。實務建議：兩個條件都掃過一次，別預設它們重合。

陷阱與對策

陷阱：「我們過了 1000 小時 125 °C 的 HTOL，所以可靠度沒問題。」 —— 這句話對 NBTI、TDDB、電遷移大致成立，對 HCI 完全不成立。 在 125 °C，HCI 的損傷率比室溫還低；元件如果會因 HCI 出事，HTOL 反而是最不容易抓到的條件。

對策：HCI 必須在晶圓層級單獨做，而且應力溫度要涵蓋規格的低溫端。 對 Grade 0 / 1 的車規元件，那是 −40 °C，不是 150 °C。 很多 qual 計畫只在高溫做，就是這樣漏掉 HCI 的 worst corner。

Where HCI sits in the standards landscape

There are many reliability standards, but only the ones below relate directly to HCI. Others (package integrity, ESD, temperature cycling, etc.) aren't relevant to HCI and aren't listed here.

LevelDocumentRole for HCI
HCI-specificJESD28
JESD28-1		 The only standard that directly governs HCI. The measurement procedure and data-analysis method for hot-carrier-induced degradation in N-channel MOSFETs under DC stress. Performed on wafer-level test structures, not finished product.
Failure modelsJEP122 Failure Mechanisms and Models for Semiconductor Devices — catalogs failure models and parameter guidance, hot carrier injection included. The industry version of Stage 4's equations.
Acceleration methodsJESD91 Methods for Developing Acceleration Models — how to legitimately extrapolate from stress conditions to use conditions. HCI's extrapolation is grounded in this class of methods.
Process-level qualificationJP-001 Foundry Process Qualification Guidelines — the primary basis for how a foundry delivers HCI data.
Product-level qualificationJESD47 Defines post-package acceptance tests like HTOL. HCI is not caught here — see the "temperature trap" below for why.
Automotive stringencyAEC-Q100 Its "chip process reliability" category covers HCI; it also defines a device's operating temperature range via temperature grades, which directly determines which temperature corner HCI stress testing needs to cover.
In one line

HCI is a "process-level" problem, not a "product-level" one. It's measured on-wafer with dedicated structures, extrapolated with a model, and handed to the design side to use. Waiting until post-package HTOL to try to catch it is both too late and, mechanistically, doesn't work.

Pass/fail criteria: what counts as "broken"

HCI doesn't make a device suddenly stop working — it's continuous parameter drift. So "failure" has to be an arbitrarily defined line.

Standard monitored parameters and lifetime definition

Four parameters are monitored: threshold voltage VTH, transconductance GM, linear-region drain current IDLIN, saturation-region drain current IDSAT.
Lifetime is defined as a 10% parameter change; for VTH, it's defined as a 50 mV change relative to the unstressed value.

Why these four parameters

ParameterSensitivity to HCIPhysical reason / corresponding circuit function
IDLINMost sensitive At low VD, the whole channel is uniformly inverted, so the damaged region at the drain sits directly in series with the current path, equivalent to extra resistance plus a local Vt shift.
Corresponding function: Ron — analog switches, sampling switches, LDO pass devices, switched-capacitor circuits.
GMSensitive Interface traps scatter carriers in the channel, lowering effective mobility.
Corresponding function: amplifier gain, GBW, slew rate.
VTHModerate The shift caused by trapped charge. Measured as an absolute value (50 mV) rather than a percentage, because it's Vt's absolute value that the circuit cares about.
Corresponding function: bias point, matched-pair offset.
IDSATInsensitive At VG=VD=high, current is set by the source-side barrier and velocity saturation; the drain end is already pinched off with low carrier density, so the damaged region contributes little.
Corresponding function: drive strength, delay, current sources.
The point most commonly misread

ΔIDLIN ≫ ΔIDSAT (commonly 3-10× in the literature) is the inevitable result of HCI's localized damage, not a device anomaly.

So "IDLIN hits its limit first while IDSAT still has plenty of margin" is normal. The right question isn't "why is IDLIN so bad," it's "which region does this device actually operate in?" — linear-region operation (switches, pass devices) should be gated on IDLIN; saturation-region operation (amplifiers, current sources) has IDSAT and GM as its functional parameters, and IDLIN isn't a functional limit for it.

Advanced trick: swap source and drain and re-measure (reverse mode), turning the damaged end into the source, which amplifies sensitivity further — the standard technique for locating the damage and confirming "the damage really is at the drain."

How stress conditions are set

The HCI stress-test procedure is, in essence, the experimental version of Stage 4's Age function:

StepWhat to doWhy
1Sweep VG at the target VD, measure the Isub–VG curve, find where it peaks Isub is the only externally, instantaneously measurable proxy for field strength
2Apply DC stress at the VG where Isub peaks, periodically interrupting to measure the four parameters (MSM: measure–stress–measure) DC conditions are repeatable and comparable; HCI never recovers, so interrupting to measure doesn't distort the result
3Repeat at multiple VD values, obtaining several lifetimes τ Establishes the voltage-acceleration relationship needed for extrapolation
4Plot ln(τ·ID/W) against ln(Isub/ID); the slope is −m m should be extracted from your own data, not assumed to be 3
5Use the Age function to extrapolate to the use condition's VD and temperature This step carries the most uncertainty — see the caveats below

Three design choices people often get grilled on

QuestionAnswer
Why DC stress,
instead of simulating a real AC waveform?		 Because HCI is quasi-static: carrier heating happens on a sub-picosecond timescale, and there's no recovery effect. So the damage from any AC waveform equals the integral of the DC damage rate along that waveform.
DC stress supplies model parameters; the AC part is left to the design side's reliability simulator, integrated along their own waveform. Model with DC, compute AC from the model — that way one dataset can serve every customer circuit.
Why test at wafer level
instead of waiting for post-package HTOL?		 Three reasons. Mechanistically it wouldn't catch it (HTOL runs at 125 °C, which decelerates HCI's negative-activation-energy behavior); it's too late (HTOL is a product-level test — by the time you find out, the process is frozen and customers have already taped out); it's the wrong deliverable (customers need a model they can plug into a reliability simulator, not a "we passed").
Why stress at peak Isub?
Is that where the damage is worst?		 Not exactly. Peak Isub is the condition where the signal is easiest to measure and the acceleration is most stable — that's why it became standard practice. But since the damage rate ∝ Isubm/IDm−1, its actual peak sits at a lower VG than peak Isub.
It's normal for the two to differ. Practical advice: sweep both conditions rather than assuming they coincide.

Temperature: HCI's own trap

This is where this whole course has really been leading. Put Stage 4's negative-activation-energy result into the context of a spec:

Wear-out mechanismActivation energy125 °C high-temp testHow to test it
HCINegative
typically −0.1 to −0.3 eV		 Actually lighter
not caught		 Wafer-level DC stress (JESD28), worst case at the low-temperature end, extrapolated with the Age function
NBTI / PBTIPositiveAccelerated Visible at both wafer level and HTOL. Has a recovery effect, so measurement delay must be controlled
TDDBPositiveAccelerated Wafer level, Weibull statistics + area extrapolation

The trap, and the fix

The trap: "We passed 1000 hours of 125 °C HTOL, so reliability is fine." — That statement roughly holds for NBTI, TDDB, and electromigration, but it doesn't hold for HCI at all. At 125 °C, HCI's damage rate is lower than at room temperature; if a device is going to have an HCI problem, HTOL is precisely the condition least likely to catch it.

The fix: HCI must be tested separately at wafer level, with the stress temperature covering the spec's low-temperature end. For Grade 0 / 1 automotive parts, that's −40 °C, not 150 °C. A lot of qual plans only test at high temperature — that's exactly how HCI's worst corner gets missed.

AEC-Q100 temperature grades — what they mean for HCI

Different locations in a vehicle see very different environments, so AEC-Q100 grades parts by operating temperature range. For HCI, what matters isn't the upper bound — it's the lower one:

Two things to note. ① AEC-Q100 specifies the stress-test items and conditions, not "how many years this part will last"; that lifetime comes from the automaker's mission profile, which the supplier uses to compute acceleration factors and set stress conditions from there. "Passed Grade 1" does not mean "good for fifteen years." ② For most other mechanisms, qual focuses on the upper temperature; for HCI, the focus is the lower bound. The same qual plan has to cover both ends.

檢查你懂了嗎

為什麼 HCI 的壽命準則 VTH 用「變化 50 mV」，其他參數卻用「變化 10%」？
因為對電路而言，Vt 的絕對漂移量才是有意義的。50 mV 的偏移在類比電路裡直接變成 offset、在數位電路裡直接改變 noise margin —— 這件事跟 Vt 原本是 0.4 V 還是 0.8 V 無關。

相對地，電流與轉導的絕對值隨元件尺寸差好幾個數量級，只有百分比才可比較。準則的形式（絕對值 vs 百分比）反映的是它對應的電路功能，不是隨便定的。
IDLIN 撞線但 IDSAT 還很寬鬆，是元件有問題嗎？
不是，這是必然結果。HCI 的損傷集中在汲極端幾十奈米。線性區量測時整條通道均勻反轉，損傷區直接串在電流路徑上（等效串聯電阻 + 局部 Vt shift）→ 極敏感；飽和區量測時電流由源極端的位障與速度飽和決定 → 不敏感。文獻常見比值 3–10 倍。

真正該問的是：這顆元件在應用裡工作在線性區還是飽和區？線性區操作（開關、pass device、Ron 是功能本身）就該卡 IDLIN；飽和區操作（放大器、電流源）的功能參數是 IDSAT 與 GM。
為什麼 HCI 應力用 DC，而不是直接模擬客戶的 AC 波形？
因為 HCI 是準靜態（載子加熱在次皮秒完成）而且沒有回復，所以任意波形的損傷等於沿波形對 DC 損傷率作積分。

因此正確的分工是：foundry 用 DC 應力建立模型參數，設計端用自己的波形去積分。一組 DC 資料可以服務所有客戶電路 —— 如果改成用某個特定 AC 波形去測，那份資料只對那個電路有效。

（順帶一提：NBTI 有回復效應，這個「DC 建模、AC 積分」的邏輯對它就不能直接套用。）
為什麼在 peak Isub 施加應力？那裡是損傷最大的地方嗎？
不完全是。peak Isub 是訊號最好量、加速最穩定、可重複性最好的條件，所以成為傳統做法。

但損傷率 ∝ Isubm/IDm−1，分母的 IDm−1 會把峰值往更低的 VG（低電流、高電場）拉。兩個峰不重合是正常的，實務上建議兩個條件都掃過，不要預設它們一樣。
客戶說「你們的元件過了 1000 小時 125 °C 的 HTOL，所以 HCI 沒問題」。這句話對嗎？
完全不對，而且是最危險的一種誤解。HTOL 靠高溫加速，這對 NBTI、TDDB、電遷移（正活化能）成立，但 HCI 的活化能是負的 —— 125 °C 的 HCI 損傷率比室溫還低。

換句話說，HTOL 對 HCI 不但不是加速測試，反而是最不容易抓到問題的條件。HCI 必須在晶圓層級用 JESD28 的方法單獨做，而且要涵蓋規格的低溫端。
身為 foundry，交給客戶的 HCI 資料應該是什麼？
是模型，不是一個壽命數字。客戶（IC 設計端）需要的是能放進 reliability simulator 的參數：Age function 的 m、Ai、Bi，加上 Ea 與它們各自的適用範圍（電壓區間、溫度區間、ΔL/L 的有效範圍）。

因為設計端的電路波形千變萬化，只有模型能讓他們沿自己的波形積分。給一句「我們的元件十年沒問題」對他們毫無用處 —— 那句話只在你假設的 mission profile 下成立。

The three-way division of labor for HCI data

RoleWhat they owe on HCIBasis
Foundry Age function parameters (m, Ai, Bi), lifetime extrapolation curves, Ea together with the temperature and bias range it applies to, and a recommended criterion. They deliver a model, not a single lifetime number. JESD28, JP-001, JEP122
IC design Take the foundry's model plus their own circuit's actual waveform, integrate it in a reliability simulator, and prove the circuit stays within spec after ten years. The PDK's reliability model
Product / QA Post-package system-level qualification and grade declarations to customers. HCI is essentially invisible at this layer — it's already been handled by the two layers before it. JESD47, AEC-Q100

The seams are where things go wrong: when the foundry's model's valid range, the design side's mission profile, and the product side's test conditions carry mismatched assumptions, nobody notices — until the return rate climbs after volume production. This is especially true for HCI, since it's the one major mechanism that "product-level testing can't see."

Check your understanding

Why does HCI's lifetime criterion for VTH use "a 50 mV change" while the other parameters use "a 10% change"?
Because for a circuit, it's Vt's absolute drift that matters. A 50 mV shift becomes an offset in an analog circuit or directly eats into noise margin in a digital circuit — regardless of whether Vt started at 0.4 V or 0.8 V.

By contrast, the absolute values of current and transconductance vary by orders of magnitude across device sizes, so only a percentage is comparable. The form of the criterion (absolute vs. percentage) reflects the circuit function it corresponds to — it's not arbitrary.
IDLIN hits its limit while IDSAT still has plenty of margin — is something wrong with the device?
No, this is the inevitable outcome. HCI's damage is concentrated in a few tens of nanometers at the drain. A linear-region measurement uniformly inverts the whole channel, putting the damaged region directly in series with the current path (equivalent series resistance + local Vt shift) → extremely sensitive; a saturation-region measurement is dominated by the source-side barrier and velocity saturation → insensitive. Ratios of 3-10× are common in the literature.

The real question is: which region does this device actually operate in? Linear-region operation (switches, pass devices, where Ron is the function itself) should be gated on IDLIN; saturation-region operation (amplifiers, current sources) has IDSAT and GM as its functional parameters.
Why does HCI stress use DC instead of directly simulating the customer's AC waveform?
Because HCI is quasi-static (carrier heating completes in sub-picoseconds) and has no recovery, so the damage from any waveform equals the integral of the DC damage rate along that waveform.

So the correct division of labor is: the foundry builds model parameters from DC stress, and the design side integrates along their own waveform. One set of DC data can serve every customer circuit — if you instead measured against one specific AC waveform, that data would only be valid for that one circuit.

(As an aside: NBTI does have a recovery effect, so this "model with DC, integrate AC" logic can't be applied to it directly.)
Why stress at peak Isub? Is that where the damage is worst?
Not exactly. Peak Isub is the condition with the easiest signal to measure, the most stable acceleration, and the best repeatability, which is why it became standard practice.

But since the damage rate ∝ Isubm/IDm−1, the IDm−1 in the denominator pulls the true peak toward a lower VG (lower current, higher field). It's normal for the two peaks not to coincide; in practice, sweep both conditions rather than assuming they're the same.
A customer says "your device passed 1000 hours of 125 °C HTOL, so HCI isn't a problem." Is that right?
Completely wrong, and one of the most dangerous misunderstandings out there. HTOL relies on high temperature for acceleration, which holds for NBTI, TDDB, and electromigration (positive activation energy), but HCI's activation energy is negative — HCI's damage rate at 125 °C is lower than at room temperature.

In other words, HTOL isn't just a non-accelerated test for HCI — it's precisely the condition least likely to catch a problem. HCI has to be tested separately at wafer level using the JESD28 method, covering the spec's low-temperature end.
As a foundry, what HCI data should you hand to customers?
A model, not a single lifetime number. The customer (the IC design side) needs parameters they can plug into a reliability simulator: the Age function's m, Ai, Bi, plus Ea and each one's valid range (voltage range, temperature range, the valid range of ΔL/L).

Because the design side's circuit waveforms vary endlessly, only a model lets them integrate along their own waveform. Telling them "our device is fine for ten years" is useless — that statement only holds under the mission profile you assumed.
