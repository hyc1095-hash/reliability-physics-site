Stage 4 / 7

Statistics & extrapolation: from 100 measured devices to a statement about a hundred million

TDDB is brutal because you can only gather data under high voltage, small area, a few hours, yet you must guarantee a failure rate below ppm at use voltage, the whole chip, ten years. Three separate extrapolations sit in between, and each one amplifies uncertainty. This page pulls the three apart.

Extrapolation ①
From 63% to 1 ppm
What you measure is near the median; what the customer wants is the earliest-failing sliver. Governed by the Weibull slope β.
Extrapolation ②
From test capacitor to whole chip
Area can differ by 10³–10⁵×. Governed by the area-scaling law — β calls the shots again.
Extrapolation ③
From stress condition to use condition
Voltage drops 2–3×, temperature drops 50 °C. Governed by the acceleration model — and picking the wrong one shifts the answer by orders of magnitude.

Extrapolation ①　How to read a Weibull distribution

分布函數
F(t) = 1 − exp[ −( t / η )β ]

F(t) 是「到時間 t 為止，累積有多少比例壞掉」。 η 是特徵壽命，代入 t = η 得 F = 1 − e−1 = 63.2%，所以常寫成 t63。 β 是形狀參數 —— 上一頁我們已經看到它就是 tox/a₀。

β 大（厚氧）大家差不多時間壞，分布集中，外推溫和
β 小（薄氧）壞的時間差很多，早夭嚴重，外推非常吃虧
Distribution function
F(t) = 1 − exp[ −( t / η )β ]

F(t) is "the cumulative fraction that has failed by time t." η is the characteristic lifetime: plugging in t = η gives F = 1 − e−1 = 63.2%, which is why it's often written t63. β is the shape parameter — the previous page already showed it's just tox/a₀.

Large β(thick oxide)Devices fail around the same time, distribution tight, extrapolation gentle
Small β(thin oxide)Failure times spread widely, early deaths severe, extrapolation very costly
把它反過來 —— 這才是工程上真正在用的式子
t(F) = η · [ −ln(1 − F) ]1/β

要問「1 ppm 的元件什麼時候壞」，就把 F = 10−6 代進去。 注意 [−ln(1−F)]1/β 這一項在 F 很小的時候是個很小的數 —— 它就是你要付的外推罰款。

Turn it around — this is the form engineers actually use
t(F) = η · [ −ln(1 − F) ]1/β

To ask "when does a 1-ppm device fail," plug in F = 10−6. Notice that the term [−ln(1−F)]1/β is a very small number when F is small — that number is the extrapolation penalty you have to pay.

外推 ②　面積縮放

Look closely at this number. At β = 5 (a 5 nm thick oxide), the 1-ppm lifetime is still about 6% of t63; at β = 1.5 (thin oxide) it drops to a few parts in ten thousand. For the same median lifetime, a thin oxide simply cannot deliver a ppm-level guarantee — which is exactly why advanced processes have to rely on area management and voltage management, not just a tougher material.

Extrapolation ②　Area scaling

最弱環節
t63(A₂) = t63(A₁) · ( A₁ / A₂ )1/β

上一頁的蒙地卡羅已經直接驗證過這條式子。 物理意義：面積放大就是同時擲更多骰子，只要任何一處先貫穿，整顆就死。 數學上，Weibull 隨機變數取最小值仍是 Weibull，β 不變、η 被壓小。

Weakest link
t63(A₂) = t63(A₁) · ( A₁ / A₂ )1/β

The previous page's Monte Carlo simulation already verified this formula directly. Physically: scaling up the area is the same as rolling more dice at once — the instant any single spot percolates through, the whole device is dead. Mathematically, the minimum of Weibull random variables is still Weibull — β stays the same, η just gets compressed.

外推 ③　加速模型 —— TDDB 最大的爭議

你在 3.6 V 量到壽命，要外推到 1.8 V。問題是：壽命隨電壓怎麼變？ 文獻上有三套主流模型，它們在應力區間幾乎重疊、都能 fit 你的資料， 但外推到使用電壓時會分家好幾個數量級。

Extrapolation ③　Acceleration models — TDDB's biggest controversy

You measure a lifetime at 3.6 V and need to extrapolate to 1.8 V. The question is: how does lifetime scale with voltage? The literature has three mainstream models. Within the stress range they nearly overlap and all fit your data equally well, but once extrapolated to use voltage, they part ways by orders of magnitude.

E-model（熱化學）

t63 ∝ exp( −γ · Eox )

電場直接把 Si–O 鍵拉開（極性鍵與電場耦合）。 外推最保守（預測壽命最短）。γ 典型 1.5～4 cm/MV（本頁取 2.0）。

1/E model（陽極電洞注入）

t63 ∝ exp( G / Eox )

損傷來自 Fowler–Nordheim 穿隧的載子， 所以跟穿隧電流一樣是 exp(−B/E) 的形式。外推最樂觀。

冪次律 V−n

t63 ∝ V−n

薄氧（< 4 nm）的實測資料在 log t – log V 上是直線， 且 n 很大（越薄越大，約 20～45；本頁取 30）。目前先進製程最常用。

E-model (thermochemical)

t63 ∝ exp( −γ · Eox )

The field directly pulls apart the Si–O bond (a polar bond coupling to the field). Its extrapolation is the most conservative (shortest predicted lifetime). γ is typically 1.5–4 cm/MV (this page uses 2.0).

1/E model (anode hole injection)

t63 ∝ exp( G / Eox )

Damage comes from carriers that Fowler–Nordheim tunnel through, so like the tunneling current itself it takes the exp(−B/E) form. Its extrapolation is the most optimistic.

Power law V−n

t63 ∝ V−n

Measured data for thin oxides (< 4 nm) forms a straight line on log t – log V, with a large n (larger for thinner oxides, roughly 20–45; this page uses 30). The most commonly used model in advanced processes today.

溫度：TDDB 的 Ea 是正的

That multiplier is TDDB's most honest measure of uncertainty. It isn't measurement error — it's disagreement between physical models. So the industry's practice is to: run long-duration stress at a low voltage as close to the use condition as practical (shortening the extrapolation distance), and state explicitly which model was used in the report. A TDDB report that never names its model is a number with no meaning.

Temperature: TDDB's Ea is positive

Arrhenius
t63 ∝ exp( Ea / kBT )　，　Ea ≈ 0.6 – 0.9 eV

缺陷生成是一個需要越過能障的鍵斷裂反應，溫度越高越容易發生 —— 所以越熱越短命。 Ea 為正，正負號合乎直覺。

Arrhenius
t63 ∝ exp( Ea / kBT )　,　Ea ≈ 0.6 – 0.9 eV

Defect generation is a bond-breaking reaction that has to cross an energy barrier — the hotter it is, the more readily it happens, so hotter means shorter-lived. Ea is positive, and the sign matches intuition.

HCI：Ea < 0
低溫更嚴重（聲子散射少 → 載子跑得更遠更熱）。 所以 125 °C 的 HTOL 抓不到 HCI，必須另外做低溫 HCI 測試。
TDDB：Ea > 0
高溫更嚴重。 所以高溫 HTOL 對 TDDB 是有效的加速測試，溫度與電壓可以同時加速，實驗時間才做得完。

同一顆晶片上兩個機制的溫度方向相反 —— 這正是為什麼可靠度驗證不能只做一個溫度點。

檢查你懂了嗎

為什麼 TDDB 規格寫「10 年 1 ppm」，而 HCI 規格寫「10 年 Idsat 退化 10%」？
因為兩者的失效型態不同。HCI 是連續參數漂移，每顆元件的退化曲線幾乎重疊，所以定一個參數門檻就夠。TDDB 是一次性崩潰，元件之間的壽命散布好幾個數量級，講「中位數壽命 10 年」等於承認有一半的產品在保固內會壞 —— 只能用失效率語言。
同一批資料，用 1/E model 報告出來的壽命比 E-model 長 10⁵ 倍。誰對？
在應力區間內兩者都對（都 fit 得很好），差別只出現在外推區，那裡沒有資料可以裁決。工程上的正確作法不是選一個相信，而是：(1) 降低應力電壓、拉長測試時間，把外推距離縮短；(2) 用最保守的模型做規格判定；(3) 報告中明確寫出模型與參數。看到沒寫模型的 TDDB 壽命數字，等於沒有數字。
把測試電容面積做大一點，可以讓 TDDB 資料更有代表性嗎？
可以，而且是好習慣。大面積電容一次涵蓋更多獨立區塊，(a) 縮短到產品面積的外推距離，(b) 更容易抓到外質缺陷（小電容常常剛好避開），(c) 在同樣的測試時間內就會失效，不必加那麼高的電壓。代價是每顆的壽命變短、量到的分布偏向早夭端，需要足夠樣本數才能同時定出 β 與 η。
溫度從 125 °C 降到 105 °C，TDDB 壽命大約增加幾倍？跟降電壓比呢？
Ea ≈ 0.75 eV 時大約 1.8 倍。相較之下，在 E-model γ = 2 cm/MV、tox = 3 nm 下，電壓只要降 0.3 V 就是 exp(2×1) ≈ 7.4 倍。電壓的槓桿遠大於溫度 —— 這就是為什麼 TDDB 的第一對策永遠是降電場，而不是散熱。

HCI: Ea < 0
Worse at low temperature (less phonon scattering → carriers travel farther and get hotter). So a 125 °C HTOL screen doesn't catch HCI — a separate low-temperature HCI test is required.
TDDB: Ea > 0
Worse at high temperature. So high-temperature HTOL is an effective accelerated test for TDDB — temperature and voltage can accelerate it together, which is what makes the test finish in reasonable time.

The two mechanisms on the same chip point in opposite temperature directions — which is exactly why reliability qualification can never rely on a single temperature point.

Check your understanding

Why does a TDDB spec say "10 years at 1 ppm," while an HCI spec says "10 years, 10% Idsat degradation"?
Because the two failure modes differ. HCI is continuous parameter drift — every device's degradation curve nearly overlaps, so setting one parameter threshold is enough. TDDB is one-shot breakdown — lifetimes scatter across orders of magnitude between devices, so saying "10-year median lifetime" would be admitting that half the product fails within warranty. Only failure-rate language works.
Using the same dataset, the 1/E model reports a lifetime 10⁵× longer than the E-model. Who's right?
Both are right within the stress range (both fit well) — the difference only shows up in the extrapolated region, where there's no data to settle it. The correct engineering approach isn't to pick one to believe, but to: (1) lower the stress voltage and lengthen the test to shorten the extrapolation distance; (2) use the most conservative model for spec decisions; (3) state the model and parameters explicitly in the report. A TDDB lifetime number with no stated model is effectively no number at all.
Does making the test capacitor's area bigger make TDDB data more representative?
Yes, and it's good practice. A larger capacitor covers more independent blocks at once: (a) it shortens the extrapolation distance to product area, (b) it more reliably catches extrinsic defects (small capacitors often just happen to miss them), (c) it fails within the same test duration without needing as high a voltage. The cost is a shorter lifetime per device and a distribution skewed toward early failures, so you need enough samples to pin down both β and η.
If temperature drops from 125 °C to 105 °C, roughly how much does TDDB lifetime increase? How does that compare to lowering voltage?
At Ea ≈ 0.75 eV, about 1.8×. By comparison, under the E-model with γ = 2 cm/MV and tox = 3 nm, dropping voltage by just 0.3 V gives exp(2×1) ≈ 7.4×. Voltage's leverage is far greater than temperature's — which is exactly why the first countermeasure for TDDB is always lowering the field, not improving heat dissipation.
