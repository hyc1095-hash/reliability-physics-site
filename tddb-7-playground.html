檢查你懂了嗎

把應力電壓從 4 V 降到 3 V 重做實驗，測試時間變成 100 倍。值得嗎？
幾乎總是值得。外推距離縮短一半，模型分歧（見上面的比較圖）通常會從幾個數量級收斂到幾倍。一個外推距離短的粗略量測，比一個外推距離長的精密量測可信得多 —— 因為誤差來源不是量測精度，是模型形式。
客戶把目標失效率從 100 ppm 收緊到 1 ppm，你的壽命掉多少？
掉 (10−6/10−4)1/β = 100−1/β 倍。β = 5 時是 0.40 倍（還好），β = 2 時是 0.10 倍（掉一個數量級），β = 1.2 時是 0.02 倍。β 越小，ppm 要求越致命。所以薄氧製程進車規的難點不在平均壽命，在 β。
上面的「還差多少」面板中，為什麼「加厚氧化層」算出來的需求偏高？
因為那個計算刻意固定了 β，只讓厚度影響電場。真實情況下加厚會同時拉大 β（階段 2：β ≈ tox/a₀），而 β 變大會同時減輕面積與失效率兩道外推的罰款 —— 三重好處。所以真實需要的厚度比面板顯示的少。這也提醒一件事：可靠度的參數彼此耦合，單獨掃一個變數會系統性地誤導。
這個工具算出來的數字可以直接放進報告嗎？
不行，而且這正是重點。本工具的 t₀、γ、n、G、Ea 都是文獻量級的示意值，不是你的製程實測值；滲流模型也是簡化的立方格點。它能給你的是因果方向與量級感：哪個旋鈕最有力、哪一道外推最痛、模型分歧有多大。真正的數字必須來自你自己的定電壓 TDDB 資料與 TCAD。

Check your understanding

You drop the stress voltage from 4 V to 3 V and re-run the experiment; test time becomes 100× longer. Worth it?
Almost always. Halving the extrapolation distance usually collapses the model divergence (see the comparison plot above) from orders of magnitude down to a few multiples. A rough measurement with a short extrapolation distance is far more trustworthy than a precise measurement with a long one — because the source of error isn't measurement precision, it's the choice of model.
The customer tightens the target failure rate from 100 ppm to 1 ppm. How much does your lifetime drop?
By a factor of (10−6/10−4)1/β = 100−1/β. At β = 5 that's 0.40× (not bad), at β = 2 it's 0.10× (a full order of magnitude), at β = 1.2 it's 0.02×. The smaller β is, the more lethal a ppm requirement becomes. That's why the hard part of reaching automotive grade for a thin-oxide process isn't the average lifetime — it's β.
In the "how far off" panel above, why does "thicken the oxide" come out needing more thickness than seems right?
Because that calculation deliberately holds β fixed and only lets thickness affect the field. In reality, thickening also raises β (Stage 2: β ≈ tox/a₀), and a larger β lightens both the area and failure-rate extrapolation penalties at the same time — a triple benefit. So the thickness actually needed is less than the panel shows. This is a reminder that reliability parameters are coupled to each other — sweeping one variable in isolation is systematically misleading.
Can the numbers this tool computes go straight into a report?
No — and that's exactly the point. This tool's t₀, γ, n, G, and Ea are all illustrative, literature-scale values, not measurements from your own process; the percolation model is also a simplified cubic lattice. What it can give you is a sense of direction and magnitude: which knob has the most leverage, which extrapolation hurts the most, how big the model divergence is. The real numbers have to come from your own constant-voltage TDDB data and TCAD.
