絕緣層為什麼會「用久了就破」

閘極氧化層只有幾個原子層厚，卻要擋住整個工作電壓十年。這一頁講它是怎麼慢慢輸掉的。

一分鐘版

電子會穿隧通過氧化層。每一次穿隧都有很小的機率打斷一個 Si–O 鍵，留下一個永久缺陷—— 就像上一頁比喻的那面磚牆，每次電子經過都有機會弄壞一塊磚。 缺陷位置是隨機的。當它們剛好連成一條從上到下的通路，電流找到捷徑 —— 崩潰。 所以崩潰不是「磨到極限」，而是等一個隨機組合湊齊。

三件同時在發生的事

步驟發生什麼
1. 穿隧 氧化層雖然是絕緣體，但只有 1–5 nm 厚。量子力學允許電子直接穿隧過去（薄氧） 或在高場下經由三角位障穿隧（Fowler–Nordheim，厚氧）。所以「絕緣層」其實一直有微弱電流通過——它從來不是完美的牆。
2. 生成缺陷 穿過去的電子帶著能量。它可能直接打斷 Si–O 鍵，也可能在陽極撞出電洞、電洞再回頭注入造成損傷 （這條路徑叫 anode hole injection）。無論哪條路徑，結果都一樣：在氧化層裡留下一個可以導電的陷阱（trap）。
3. 連成通路 陷阱位置隨機分布。電子可以在相鄰的陷阱之間跳躍導電。當陷阱多到剛好形成一條從閘極貫穿到基板的鏈， 就出現一條低阻抗路徑 —— 電流暴衝、局部發熱、氧化層熔穿。這就是崩潰。

為什麼這件事「本質上是隨機的」

下面是一片氧化層的側視圖，每個小方格代表一小塊氧化層。拖滑桿改變厚度與電場，按「開始」讓缺陷隨機長出來，看它們什麼時候剛好連成一條路。 每按一次重來，崩潰的時間都不一樣 —— 這不是模擬的雜訊，這就是物理本身。

Stage 1 / 7 · Intuition

Why insulation "wears out and breaks"

A gate oxide is only a few atomic layers thick, yet it has to hold off the full operating voltage for ten years. This page covers how it slowly loses that fight.

The one-minute version

Electrons tunnel through the oxide. Every tunneling event has a small probability of breaking a Si–O bond, leaving behind a permanent defect — just like the brick-wall analogy from the previous page: every electron that passes has a chance of damaging one brick. Defect locations are random. The instant they happen to connect into a path from top to bottom, current finds a shortcut — breakdown. So breakdown isn't "wearing down to a limit" — it's waiting for a random combination to line up.

Three things happening at once

StepWhat happens
1. Tunneling The oxide is an insulator, but it's only 1–5 nm thick. Quantum mechanics lets electrons tunnel straight through (thin oxide) or tunnel through a triangular barrier under high field (Fowler–Nordheim, thicker oxide). So an "insulator" always carries a faint current — it was never a perfect wall.
2. Defect generation Electrons that tunnel through carry energy. They can directly break a Si–O bond, or knock loose a hole at the anode that then injects back and causes damage (this path is called anode hole injection). Either way, the result is the same: a conductive trap is left behind in the oxide.
3. Connecting into a path Trap locations are randomly distributed. Electrons can hop conductively between neighboring traps. Once there are enough traps to happen to form a chain from the gate all the way to the substrate, a low-impedance path appears — current surges, local heating, the oxide melts through. That's breakdown.

最重要的一句話

TDDB 不是「磨損到極限」，而是「等一個隨機組合湊齊」。 兩顆一模一樣的元件、跑一模一樣的條件，壞掉的時間可以差好幾倍。

這就是為什麼 TDDB 的規格不能寫「壽命 10 年」，只能寫 「十年後累積失效率低於 X ppm，信心水準 Y%」。統計不是附註，統計就是規格本身。

檢查你懂了嗎

為什麼氧化層越薄，反而是「一開始就有電流」而不是完全絕緣？
因為量子穿隧的機率隨厚度指數下降。5 nm 以上時穿隧電流可以忽略；到了 2 nm 以下，直接穿隧電流大到會影響待機功耗 —— 這正是先進製程要改用 high-k 的原因之一（用較厚的物理厚度達到同樣的等效電容）。
既然缺陷是隨機的，為什麼厚一點的氧化層反而「比較好預測」？
因為要貫穿一層厚氧化層，需要更多缺陷剛好排成一列。需要的條件越苛刻，各顆元件之間的差異反而越小（相對變異變小）—— 統計上就是 Weibull 斜率 β 變大、分布變窄。

反過來，超薄氧化層只要兩三個缺陷就能連通，運氣成分極大、分布極寬。階段 2 的模擬會把這件事直接跑出來給你看。
同樣的電壓，把面積放大十倍，崩潰會提早嗎？
會。面積越大，等於同時擲更多次骰子 —— 只要任何一處形成貫穿路徑，整顆元件就完了。

這是 TDDB 特有的性質（HCI 沒有）：壽命隨面積縮放。所以在小面積測試電容上量到的漂亮數據，外推到整顆晶片的閘極總面積時會大幅縮水。階段 4 會算給你看。

The one sentence that matters most

TDDB is not "wearing down to a limit" — it's "waiting for a random combination to line up." Two identical devices, run under identical conditions, can fail at times that differ by several times over.

That's why a TDDB spec can never say "10-year lifetime" — it can only say "cumulative failure rate below X ppm after ten years, at Y% confidence." Statistics isn't a footnote here; statistics is the spec itself.

Check your understanding

Why does a thinner oxide mean there's already a current flowing from the start, rather than perfect insulation?
Because tunneling probability drops exponentially with thickness. Above 5 nm, tunneling current is negligible; below 2 nm, direct tunneling current gets large enough to affect standby power — this is one reason advanced processes switch to high-k (a physically thicker layer gives the same equivalent capacitance).
If defects are random, why is a thicker oxide actually "more predictable"?
Because breaking through a thicker oxide requires more defects to happen to line up. The stricter the requirement, the smaller the relative variation between devices — statistically, that means a larger Weibull slope β and a narrower distribution.

Conversely, an ultra-thin oxide can be bridged by just two or three defects, so luck dominates and the distribution is very wide. Stage 2's simulation will show you this directly.
Same voltage, ten times the area — does breakdown happen sooner?
Yes. A larger area is like rolling more dice at once — the moment any single spot forms a percolation path, the whole device is done for.

This is a property unique to TDDB (HCI doesn't have it): lifetime scales with area. So the clean numbers measured on a small test capacitor shrink substantially once extrapolated to a whole chip's total gate area. Stage 4 works out the numbers.
