Hot Carrier Injection（HCI）
A high field accelerates carriers into "hot" carriers, knocks loose electron-hole pairs, and injects some of them into the gate oxide, leaving interface traps. Device parameters drift continuously: Vt shifts, Id drops, gm drops.

Gate dielectric · random breakdown

Time-Dependent Dielectric Breakdown

Time-Dependent Dielectric Breakdown（TDDB）
Defects accumulate randomly inside the oxide until they happen to link into one percolating path — then it shorts out, suddenly. No warning, only probability. The entire toolkit (Weibull, area scaling, ppm) is unlike HCI.

Bias Temperature Instability

NBTI / PBTI
Under negative gate bias and high temperature, a pMOS's Vt keeps drifting — and partially recovers once the stress is removed. "It's already recovering while you measure it" is what makes this mechanism so tricky. Directly at odds with TDDB's nitridation countermeasure.
5 stagesFailure mode: continuous drift + recoveryEa: positive Coupled with both HCI and TDDB

Metal interconnect · mass transport

Electromigration

Electromigration（EM）
Electron momentum pushes metal atoms along, eventually opening a void at the cathode end and squeezing out a hillock at the anode end. Black's equation, a material activation-energy table, Blech length, and statistics that are lognormal rather than Weibull.
